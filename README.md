# Hoops Career

A mobile-first basketball GM **and** head-coach game, built as an installable offline PWA with Capacitor shells for Android and iOS. It starts in the 2026-27 season with real rosters (New League also offers a fictional league with the real franchises), and its main feature is a tactics layer that tells you _why_ things happen.

## What's in the box

**Possession-by-possession engine** (`src/core/sim/engine.ts`)

- Every half-court trip runs a concrete action: pick-and-roll, isolation, post-up, handoffs/off-ball screens, or motion/cuts/drive-and-kick. Transition, putbacks, presses and end-of-game situations are modeled too.
- Defense chooses its pick-and-roll coverage (drop, switch, hedge, blitz, ice), base scheme (man, 2-3, 3-2), help level, ball pressure, physicality, transition defense, press and a star plan (deny or double).
- Each tactical effect is tagged with its expected-points value (e.g. "drop coverage conceded pull-ups: +3.4"). That is what drives the post-game report, live assistant tips and the Tactics Lab.
- Rotations follow a minute-by-minute stint plan built from your depth chart. The staff overrides it for fouls, fatigue, injuries and blowouts (hard minute caps hold: a capped player sits down up to 40 seconds early, since subs wait for a dead ball), and playoff rotations shorten to about eight (the starters and closers always among them). The starters open both halves, and the closers finish the game; the coach's starting and closing fives take the floor whatever their positions. A fouled-out or injured player is replaced by whoever the staff wants on the floor at that point, so he isn't subbed straight back out. A reserve who starts for an injured or resting starter takes over that starter's minutes. Garbage time empties the bench in real blowouts only: the starters come straight back if the game gets within reach, and nobody subs in a whole new five in the last half minute. You can also sub manually during live games.
- NBA rules at the edges of the game: teams get a shot off before the buzzer (a team tied or down three or less gets one off with a second or two left; a team protecting a late 4th-quarter lead runs out the clock instead), the shot clock runs from the inbound (breaking a press uses some of it), hack-a-player stops in the last two minutes of every period, trailing teams foul only while a foul can still buy another possession, at most two timeouts after the 3:00 mark of the 4th, and short-handed teams never play four (a player with six fouls stays in when nobody is left, and every further foul is a technical; an injured player with no healthy sub is replaced by the last player disqualified). Box scores add up to exactly 48 minutes a team (plus 5 per overtime), and the starters are the five who took the floor for the tip.
- AI coaches adjust during games: at quarter breaks and timeouts, how often they change coverage, help, pressure or the glass depends on their adjustments rating.
- Calibrated to 2025-26 league averages: pace ≈ 100, ORtg ≈ 114, ~41% of shots from three, about 27 assists per team game with NBA-like assisted shares, and turnover, rebounding, foul and block rates within 5%. Games vary like the NBA's (a margin SD near 13 for a given matchup, about 17% decided by 20+, 4-5% to overtime, the two scores moving together), and team quality spreads the standings: team net ratings track what the players' real box plus-minus (BPM) predicts at the minutes they get (r ≈ 0.88), and win SD is about 11.5 (the NBA's is about 12). Tactic extremes have real costs, so no slider setting is a free win. A full season sims in about 8 s on a laptop.

**Coaching & understanding**

- **Tactics**: playbooks with live "fit" scores for your roster, every slider explained in place, and Simple/Advanced/Expert depth. Expert mode adds roles, shot freedom, screeners and late-game rules.
- **Tactics Lab**: runs real engine games in a Web Worker. _Project_ your plan against any opponent, _Compare_ before vs after your edits (each possession replays from the same random stream, so the difference is the tactics; verdicts need two standard errors), or let the assistant generate _Counters_ and test each one (the verdict range widens with the number of counters tested, so one that changes nothing rarely looks like a win).
- **Live game**: an animated 2D court driven by the engine's possession logs, plus timeouts, subs, in-game tactic changes, and assistant alerts ("their P&R ball handler plays are scoring 1.35 per possession: drop is giving their handlers room", with a Switch to Hedge button). The play-by-play, the live box score and the On the floor strip follow the court instead of running ahead of it, skipping a quarter stops at a break panel (End of Q1, Halftime) with what still applies, and the speed you pick sticks.
- **Post-game "Why"**: the ranked factors that decided the game, with point values and advice (advice is only given when it still applies to your current tactics, and names the controls as the Tactics screen does). Also four factors, play-type PPP, lineups, player grades, shot charts, score flow, momentum swings (when, and from what score) and play-by-play by period.
- **Rotation**: reorderable depth chart, minute targets, caps, closers, rest on back-to-backs, and a 48-minute stint-plan visual.

**League systems**

- **Calendar**: the real 2026-27 schedule (82 games, 41 at home; a Cup quarterfinal or semifinal host plays one more at home, its opponent one fewer); later seasons and fictional leagues get a generated NBA-format one with no four games in five nights, no games on Thanksgiving or Christmas Eve, the All-Star Game on a Sunday and the trade deadline the Thursday ten days before it. Playoff games are every other day with breaks between rounds, so the Finals end in June; the summer has dates too (the draft in late June, free agency from June 30).
- **Competition**: the NBA Cup, standings with NBA tiebreakers (multi-team ties broken as a group; once the regular season is over every seed is marked), play-in and 2-2-1-1-1 playoffs. The news log keeps about a year of results, awards and transactions; injuries make the news from 10 games out (5 for a star).
- **Honors**: All-Star selection by the NBA format (at least four guards and six frontcourt players per conference) and game, full awards (MVP, DPOY, ROY, 6MOY, MIP, COY, All-NBA, All-Defense, All-Rookie, Finals MVP; the 65-game rule, 50 games for Sixth Man, Most Improved only for players with last season's numbers to improve on, and the League hub's award ladders use the same rules as the vote), history and Hall of Fame. An All-Star is replaced only when an injury keeps him out more than two games.
- **Player movement**: the 2019-odds draft lottery (teams on the same record split the odds of the places they share), generated draft classes with combine data, re-signing, a 30-day free agency and trades (validation, AI valuation, "what would make this work?", AI offers).
- **Players**: morale and role expectations, injuries and load management (worn-down players get hurt more; assistant-run playbooks refit when a long injury starts or ends), and development (players peak around 26-27 and fade faster past 35; training focus trades skills without costing OVR; coaching, practice intensity and minutes). Every player on a team wears his own number, no two active players share a name, and no two head coaches do.
- **Generated players** (draft classes and fictional leagues) get hidden tendencies like the real players' calibrated ones, drawn from how those relate to level and size, and every player's tendencies follow his level each offseason (a draftee who becomes a star takes a star's shots; an aging star hands them on), while a real player's calibrated usage settles toward his level's as his teammates change, so a long career keeps the real league's shot diet and spread: twelve seasons in, about 37 threes a game, a win SD near 10 and a top scorer near 40. Generated players come in the real league's shares of each position. Fictional leagues are rated on the real league's spread and built with real rosters' heights by roster spot and age mix, so their players develop and age much like real ones (they peak about a year earlier, near 26).
- **Business**: finances that grow with the cap as salaries do, revenue sharing (each team pays or receives 40% of its gap to the league's average local revenue), and owner job security (a fired coach has to take over another team before the league moves on).

**Simplified CBA**

- **Cap and tax**: a soft cap (free agents need cap space unless signed for the minimum), a non-taxpayer mid-level exception (once a season, up to 8.5% of the cap for up to 4 years, only while staying under the tax; afterwards trades can't take the team past the tax line, though minimum signings can), a luxury tax (half of it shared among the teams that didn't pay) and a salary floor.
- **Contracts**: unlimited re-signing of your own players with cap holds on them until you re-sign or renounce them or free agency opens, player and team options decided after the draft (you choose on your own team options), rookie scale, and dead money for releases (minimum deals signed for the coming season aren't guaranteed until opening night).
- **Rosters**: 13-15 players in season (up to 21 in the offseason, free agency included; draft picks can take you past 21, and then signings, re-signings included, and player-adding trades wait until you cut back; after the season only next season's contracts count, for two-way slots and two-way conversions too), plus up to three two-way contracts outside the roster and the cap.
- **Trades**: 125% + $100K salary matching when over the cap, protected draft picks and the Stepien rule. Players on expiring deals can't be traded after the season, nor can a player whose option is undecided until he decides after the draft. A signing can't be traded until December 15, or three months after an in-season signing; a team can't take back a player or pick it traded away the same season, not even through another team; and a team that used the mid-level can't take its payroll past the tax line in a trade.
- **Free agency**: free agents can be signed in preseason, the regular season and free agency, and unsigned free agents lower their price every few days, down to what a team that wants them can still pay, until someone bites.

**NBA Cup** (`src/core/league/cup.ts`): each season the teams are drawn into six groups of five from pots by last season's record, and each team plays four group games on Cup nights in November (Tuesdays and Fridays, the last one the day after Thanksgiving). The three group winners and the best runner-up in each conference reach the December knockouts: quarterfinals and semifinals at the better seed, then a championship on a neutral floor. Group, quarterfinal and semifinal games count in the standings; the championship counts in neither the standings nor season stats. As in the NBA, 80 games per team are scheduled up front with the knockout window kept clear, and once the groups are decided the teams that didn't advance (and quarterfinal losers) get their last games there, so everyone plays 82. Group ties break on head-to-head, point differential and points scored in group games, then last season's record. The League hub has a Cup tab (group tables, bracket, championship); the winner, runner-up and Cup MVP go into awards and history.

AI front offices pick a strategy by where their roster ranks (about ten contenders, ten rebuilders) and blend in their record as the season goes, so a contender at 10-30 starts selling before the deadline. They re-sign within a budget that fits that strategy, trade among themselves a few times a season (both sides have to like the deal, and nobody sends away more than two firsts in one trade), value consolidation and salary dumps honestly, put second-round picks on two-way deals, bring a clearly better free agent into summer camp even with a full roster, and make preseason cuts by value. On casual difficulty their trade discount applies to your own players and picks, not ones you just traded for, so a chain of trades can't compound it. Their strategy lens only adds a premium for what they take in: an AI team never sells you its own players or picks below their neutral value, and anything that changed hands this season is valued at face value, so flipping a veteran from a rebuilder to a contender isn't free money. A pick's value moves smoothly toward its draft, and the pick on the clock is worth the prospect it will become. "What would make this work?" adds picks and players together, sends a player when your roster is full, and says whether salary or value was the problem.

stats.nba.com, ESPN and Basketball Reference block many cloud IPs, so everything except the Basketball Reference pages comes from GitHub-hosted mirrors or NBA.com's S3 bucket, which download from anywhere.

```bash
npm run data:fetch       # Basketball Reference, ~90 pages (drafts back to 2003) at 1 request / 3.7 s, cached in data/raw/ (-- --refresh re-fetches contracts and current rosters)
npm run data:fetch-nba   # NBA.com play-by-play + matchups (3 seasons) and college box scores, ~90 MB
npm run data:fetch-extra # schedule, pick ownership, injuries, two-way contracts → data/raw/extra/ (-- --force to refresh)
npm run data:build       # parse → profile → RAPM → derive → calibrate in the engine → extras → src/data/seed.json
npm run data:extras      # refresh only the schedule / picks / injuries / two-way part of the current seed
```

Or let GitHub do it: run the **Build seed data** workflow (Actions tab → Run workflow). It fetches everything on GitHub's runners, builds and calibrates the seed, verifies the app still builds and passes its tests, commits `src/data/seed.json` to `main` and then runs CI and the Android APK build on it. It also keeps the Basketball Reference pages as a tarball on the `data-raw` branch, so later runs only fetch what's missing, plus the contracts and current rosters, which change all summer and are always fetched again. Its options:

- **commit_seed** (on by default): untick it to only build the seed and upload it as the run's `seed` artifact. A seed that differs from the committed one only in its timestamps isn't committed either way.
- **extras_only**: just refresh the injuries, pick ownership, two-way contracts and schedule in the current seed (no recalibration).
- **refetch**: ignore the pages kept on `data-raw` and fetch all of them again.

If an extra source, or a required Basketball Reference page (contracts, per-game or advanced stats), can't be downloaded, the workflow fails and leaves the committed seed alone. Locally, `data:fetch-extra` only warns: `data:extras` then keeps the seed's current version of that part, and `data:build` falls back to the game's generated version.

The current seed has 544 players: 483 under contract for 2026-27 plus the 61 unsigned players who played 250+ minutes in 2025-26 (the build takes up to 140, most minutes first). 26 players are on two-way deals (23 of them were unsigned in the contracts table, so they join their two-way teams). Six rows of the contracts table are waived players their teams still pay (Ricky Rubio, Didi Louzada, Taj Gibson and three more): the game books that pay as dead money, and those who played in the seed's seasons start as free agents. All 802 NBA.com players from the last three seasons are matched to their Basketball Reference ids. The committed seed was built before the pipeline fetched draft pages from before 2006 or recorded undrafted players' entry classes, so 96 players (LeBron James and 95 undrafted players) arrive without draft data: the game counts their NBA seasons from the stats on record, or from a debut at 22 for veterans who were already playing when the record begins (VanVleet 10), treats undrafted 2026 rookies as rookies, and numbers second-round picks within the round. The pipeline now fetches draft pages back to 2003 and records each undrafted player's entry class, so a rebuilt seed carries the real values.

What's real on opening night, besides the rosters and ratings:

- **Schedule**: the NBA's 2026-27 calendar, from opening night (Oct 20: BOS@DET, PHI@NYK, OKC@SAS) to Apr 11, with the real All-Star break (the trade deadline is the Thursday ten days before the All-Star Game, as in generated seasons). Later seasons, and fictional leagues, use the generated schedule.
- **NBA Cup**: the real groups (East A: BKN, DET, MIL, ORL, TOR …) and their 60 group games on the real Cup nights (Oct 30 to Nov 27), with quarterfinals Dec 4-5, semifinals Dec 8 and the championship Dec 11. The knockout matchups and the two games for each team that doesn't reach them are decided by the simulated group stage, as the NBA will do. If a seed has no Cup data, the missing games go on open December dates instead and there is no Cup that season.
- **Draft picks**: RealGM's ownership rows for 2027-2033 (`scripts/data/extra/picks.ts`). Each pick gets exactly one owner. Pools ("less favorable of MIL and NOP") and swap rights are resolved on a projected draft order (team strength from the seed's ratings, regressed toward the middle for later years), and conditions that depend on other picks ("if DAL conveys its 2027 first", "if not already settled") are checked against picks already resolved. A pick that conveys only outside the top N ("3-30 to Charlotte") is stored as a top-N protected pick. Where several outcomes are possible, the most likely owner gets the pick. The build prints every team's first-rounders and compares them with LD Sport's table.
- **Injuries**: players listed out on opening night start injured. The time out is counted in their team's real games before the expected return date, and a return after the regular season costs playoff games too. ESPN's description is mapped to the game's injury types where one fits (Torn ACL, Hamstring Strain...), otherwise kept descriptive (Torn Labrum). Injuries are off when the league's injury setting is off.
- **Two-way contracts** are marked and don't count toward the 15-man roster.
- **Positions** come from each player's box-score position in his latest season (Basketball Reference's current roster pages only say G / F / C). Rookies without one get theirs from the G/F/C label, size and skills: a guard who handles and passes like a lead guard for his size is a PG, a forward who handles more and rebounds less is an SF.

How ratings are made (`scripts/data/`):

1. **Play-by-play profiles** (`nba/pbp.ts`): one pass over each season builds every player's shot diet by zone (the engine's zones: rim, paint, mid-range, corner three, above-the-break three) with make rates, how he creates (pull-ups, drives, cuts, post moves, putbacks, transition, share of makes assisted), assists by the zone they set up, turnover and foul types, fouls drawn, true rebound rates from on-court opportunities, and rim deterrence (opponents' rim attempts and rim FG% with him on the floor). It also rebuilds every lineup stint.
2. **RAPM** (`nba/rapm.ts`): ridge-regressed adjusted plus-minus over about 200,000 stints from three seasons (recency-weighted, home court modeled), split into offense and defense. Matchup data (`nba/matchups.ts`) adds points saved per 100 possessions guarded, relative to what the same scorers did against everyone else.
3. **Attributes** (`derive.ts`): seasons are weighted by recency and minutes (2025-26 ×1.0, 2024-25 ×0.6, 2023-24 ×0.3), with Bayesian priors on percentages. Finishing, floaters and mid-range come from zone make rates; handling from self-creation and lost-ball rate; passing from assist and bad-pass rates; rebounding from true rebound rates; defense from RAPM, matchup defense, rim deterrence, steals and blocks.
4. **Engine-in-the-loop calibration** (`calibrate.ts`): seasons are simulated repeatedly and each player's simulated profile is compared with his real one. Shooting ratings and hidden per-player tendencies are nudged until he reproduces it. The tendencies cover usage, three-point rate, rim, mid-range and corner-three shares, self-creation, passing, turnovers, share of rebounds, steals, blocks and fouls. The tendencies absorb role and opportunity, so they don't distort the visible ratings. To keep them from overfitting one context, half the calibration games use next season's rosters and half the 2025-26 teams the stats came from, each game draws the coach's playbook from his top three, and players randomly miss games so rotations get re-planned around them.
5. **Rookies and small samples**: 2026 draftees are shaped by their college production (shooter, passer, rim protector...) and leveled to their draft slot and reputation. Short NBA samples are shrunk toward the level their minutes suggest; calibration then settles again.
6. **OVR** is a fitted, size-aware blend of attributes. The weights were fitted so that real players' ratings predict half regressed box plus-minus and half RAPM, plus the minutes and usage coaches gave them (OVR vs BPM r ≈ 0.88, vs RAPM r ≈ 0.76).

`npm run real-check` simulates a season with the real league and compares every regular with his real 2025-26 numbers. With the current seed, sim-vs-real correlations over 225 regulars are 0.91 for points, 0.91 for assists, 0.94 for rebounds, 0.92 for blocks, 0.88 for threes made, 0.84 for steals, 0.82 for minutes per game and 0.70 for true shooting (averages over three seeds). Minutes follow because coaches remember last season's roles, counted once and converted to full-strength minutes, so real rotations carry over without burying healthy veterans. Every league per-game rate (pace, points, threes, free throws, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers, fouls) lands within 5% of the 2025-26 NBA, and shot diets match too: about 0.93 for rim share, 0.88 for mid-range share and 0.92 for three-point share. It also prints the game shape (margin spread, blowouts, overtime, win spread), each team's net rating against its players' real box plus-minus, the leaders, single-game highs and the DPOY ladder, so outliers are easy to spot.

The pipeline is covered by parser and pipeline unit tests (CSV, RAPM, name matching), an offline end-to-end fixture and seed sanity tests. The fixture writes Basketball Reference-style pages for a simulated fictional league, so the build (parse, derive, calibrate) runs offline in about a minute and a half:

```bash
npx tsx scripts/data/test/make-fixture.ts /tmp/fx && HC_RAW_DIR=/tmp/fx HC_SEED_OUT=/tmp/fx-seed.json npx tsx scripts/data/build-seed.ts
```

The data scripts read a few environment variables: `HC_RAW_DIR` (where the raw downloads live, default `data/raw`; the NBA.com and extras folders inside it follow unless `HC_NBA_DIR` or `HC_EXTRA_DIR` point elsewhere), `HC_SEED_OUT` (the seed that `data:build` and `data:extras` write, default `src/data/seed.json`), `HC_REPORT` (`data:build` also writes a per-player JSON report there: minutes, advanced stats, RAPM, ratings and tendencies) and `HC_PICK_DEBUG` (a pick such as `2027:1:DAL`, whose competing ownership claims the picks resolver prints).

Visual QA: `node scripts/qa/tour.mjs <devServerUrl> <outDir> [stages]` screenshots every screen and tab on a phone and a tablet at six points of a season (preseason, trade deadline, after the playoffs, draft, re-sign period, free agency; a stage list stops after the last one asked for). ## Ship to phones

- **PWA**: deploy `dist/` to any static host and use "Add to Home Screen". It works offline after the first load, and saves stay on the device (IndexedDB, with persistent storage requested). Saves are incremental (game results live in their own store and only new ones are written; older ratings snapshots keep just OVR and potential, so a long career grows by about 0.4 MB a season), a failed save says so, offers a backup and keeps retrying (a brand-new league included), switching leagues keeps the open one's unsaved changes, and a league open in two windows can't overwrite itself. A new version downloads in the background and applies on the next launch, never mid-game; reloading after an update or from the error screen saves the league first.
- **Backups**: Settings → Export backup saves the league as a JSON file (the share sheet in the app, a download on the web). Import it from Settings or the start screen on any device.
- **Android**: the _Android APK_ workflow builds a debug APK on every push to `main` (download it from the run's artifacts). Pushing a `v*` tag attaches the APK to a release. Locally: `npm run cap:sync && npx cap open android`. The native shell adds the hardware Back button (closes the topmost sheet first, goes Home when there is no earlier screen, saves before leaving), haptics, edge-to-edge insets and no service worker (the files ship in the APK).
- **Android signing**: Android only installs an update signed with the same key as the installed app. Without signing secrets, each workflow run signs with a throwaway debug key, so a new build only installs after uninstalling the old one, which deletes its saves (export a backup first). To sign every build with one key, create a keystore once and keep it safe (a lost key means users must reinstall):
  ```bash
  keytool -genkeypair -v -keystore release.keystore -alias hoops -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000
  base64 -w0 release.keystore   # paste the output into ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64
  ```
  Then add four repository secrets (Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions): `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` (`hoops` above) and `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`. Debug APKs are then signed with that key, and `v*` tags also get a signed release APK (`app-release.apk`) on the release. The version code is the workflow's run number, so each build counts as newer than the last.
- **iOS**: `npm run cap:sync && npx cap open ios` on a Mac with Xcode.
- **GitHub Pages**: run the _Deploy PWA to Pages_ workflow manually after setting Settings → Pages → Source to "GitHub Actions". It builds for the site's own path (`/<repo>/` for a project site, `/` for a user site or a custom domain). Pages on a private repo needs a paid GitHub plan.

## Design decisions

- Ratings are shown on a 2K-style 40-99 scale, fully visible. OVR is anchored to the league's distribution, so 93+ is an MVP candidate, 87+ an All-Star, 82+ a quality starter and about 74 the median rostered player. Attributes use the same 40-99 scale (league median ≈ 68).
- The contracts table includes camp deals, so some teams start with 16-20 players. AI teams make preseason cuts down to 15 standard contracts at league creation (two-way contracts don't count). If your team is over, the Assistant GM suggests who to release before opening night.
- Free agents and AI trades are tuned for casual play by default (difficulty can be changed in Settings).
- Future picks start with their real owners (2027-2033). The game's pick model is one owner per pick plus an optional top-N protection, so RealGM's conditions are simplified: swaps and "more/less favorable of" pools go to the projected outcome, protections that send the pick to a third team, or roll over to a later year, are approximated as "stays with the original team that year, obligation ends", and picks the NBA has forfeited stay with their original team.
- The real schedule is used for 2026-27 only. Games the NBA hasn't published yet are filled on open December dates.
- Team logos are original monograms in each franchise's colors (no trademarked artwork).
