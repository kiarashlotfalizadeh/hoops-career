# 09 — The Crossover Newcomer
## Who I am and what I played
- Persona: 19, plays 2K MyNBA, opened Basketball GM once, never played FM. Took every default.
- Career A: Real 2026-27 league, default team (Washington Mages, WAS), GM + Head Coach, Teaching on, all toggles default. One full season (35-47, lost the play-in), lottery, draft, re-sign, free agency, into 2027-28 preseason, then Sim 1 month (Oct 26 / 1-2 at the stop).
- Career B: Fictional league, default team (WAS again), same defaults. 21 games (11-9, 6th East) at Nov 27.
- Live games coached: 3 (opener vs MIL, play-in @IND, fictional opener vs OKC). Two were partial, finished with Skip ahead / Sim to final.
- Device: 390x844 phone. Time: about 1.5 to 2 hours of play, plus a lot of waiting on the live-game clock.

## Play log
1. **Start screen.** Big "Hoops Career" title, three stats, one button. Clean. Career A was created in 0.9 s, B in 2 s. Zero friction.
2. **Team pick.** WAS was already pre-selected, with the team's top three players shown. I didn't know what "Large market", "roster rank 21st of 30" or "Owner: 27 wins · rebuild · frugal" meant for me. Step 3 then says "builder", not "frugal" — small contradiction.
3. **Today screen, Oct 20.** The guide queue ("Set your rotation / Pick a playbook / Plan for your opener / Coach a game live") is the best thing I met all hour. Each step is one tap with a "Keep these five / Keep this playbook / No plan this time" button. I never felt stranded. But I still had no idea what a "playbook" was until I read it ("Balanced Attack · Drop & Protect"), and the tactics board has "Post Hub", "Rim Runner", "Drop coverage" with no explanation in place.
4. **Game 1 live (Oct 21 vs MIL).** The live view is gorgeous and readable: "MIL on a 6-0 run, a timeout ends it", a "Staff" card, a coach named Derrick Lockhart talking to me. I hit "Not now" on the run calls by script. Real time for Q1 was about 3 min at 1x, so a full game would be 12+ min. Q1 ended 34-17 down. The end-of-quarter report ("What's deciding it": turnovers 7-0, "shots fell below looks, luck") is excellent. I changed Drop to Switch. The game got worse. Final 132-92, a 40-point loss in my first game. The report said Tactics -0.6, Shooting luck -25.9. Honest, but the first game as a newcomer was a flogging, and "luck, nothing to change" is cold comfort.
5. **First week.** Sim 1 week took 3.4 s. Record 1-4. The "While you were away" sheet read like a newspaper (routs, buzzer beaters, "First win for the new coach").
6. **Nov 28, 10-9.** Won 5 straight (Young scored 43 and 37). First trade offer: DEN want Coulibaly (76, 22yo) for Holmes 72 + Nnaji 67 + a 2030 1st. The card explained the effect on my rotation ("Mann 0 → 8 min") and called it Fair value. I turned it down because the owner wants young players. "TO/PO" on contracts meant nothing to me until I guessed.
7. **Feb 6 trade deadline, 23-29.** Staff call: "sell to Philadelphia" = trade Anthony Davis (87) for Jaylen Brown 85 + Edwards + three 2nds. It then said "the forecast barely moves your postseason chances". I stood pat. I could not tell why they would recommend that. I then went 9-18 and lost 5 straight to finish 35-47.
8. **Play-in @IND.** Elimination game, down 126-137 with 3:04 left, 2% to win. Used Sim to final. Lost. Season ended 35-47, one game out of the playoffs.
9. **Owner's review, Jun 7.** "I am proud of them. Tell them I said so." Grade +32. Goals: 26 wins (got 35), young minutes (11,049 vs 10,000), under tax ($9.5M under). Great story beat. The goals were low (27 → 26 wins) and I was never at risk.
10. **Lottery and draft.** The lottery reveal is fun ("Reveal the order": 14 to 5 animates). My 35-47 team picked #3, which looked wildly lucky (listed as the 10th-worst record), and I could not tell why. I took the staff's pick, Drew Hughes (SF, 76 / POT 86), then auto-drafted the rest. Drafting felt genuinely easy and good. Scouting jargon ("Unwatched ±2 OVR · ±4 POT", "proj. #4-8") is dense.
11. **Re-sign.** This is where I felt dumb. "Bird rights", "Cap / Tax / Their asks", "Keep everyone $233.3M, tax ≈ $40.1M", and Ayton: "his rights let you pay $9.7M at most. Re-signing can't keep him". I understood only because the warnings were in plain sentences. Exercising team options was easy. Declining Mann asks "Keep the option / Decline" with a clear "This can't be undone." Re-signing Coulibaly: slider plus "A little less may do" and a two-tap confirm. Nice. But the toast later said he signed for "5 years, $96.1M" when my confirm sheet said "5 yrs · $102.1M".
12. **Free agency.** DiVincenzo (78, Spacing C → B) looked ideal for my need, but the sheet warned "tax +$17.4M · crosses the tax line", which would break the owner's goal. That one warning saved me from a bad move. I signed nobody. The Play button was labelled "Open free agency" in options but my script's click found it disabled, and the actual button was on the Re-sign page; a player would have found "Re-sign players" on the Continue bar and been unsure where FA was.
13. **2027-28.** Owner now wants 34 wins and the play-in. Roster: Dybantsa 82/89, Sarr 85/90, Hughes 76/85. Young out 24 games with a foot fracture in game 1; the "Ride it out / Look for a trade" call was fair.
14. **Fictional league.** Same default, WAS, "win-now, 45 wins". Opener vs OKC. OKC shot 86.8% eFG on looks worth 62%, flagged as luck. I won 122-116. My Switch change scored "Your moves -0.7 pts", so the game told me my halftime tinkering hurt. I like that. 16 games later: "Nov 11 · The young players' minutes for the owner's goal, the staff's lean on a card you left on Oct 21. Seems to hurt." A card I left? I had no idea there was a card.

## What hooked me (top 5, each with evidence)
1. **The guide queue.** Four steps with one-tap "Keep these" answers. I was playing a game within 3 minutes of opening it.
2. **The explanations.** Every result gives a reason: "Washington's shots fell below their looks ... luck", "Your moves -0.7 pts", "Test-simmed Ice in place of Drop ... no clear edge, nothing changed". It makes me feel the game is on my side.
3. **The deal and contract screens.** The DEN offer card (what changes in my rotation, payroll after, "Fair value"), and the DiVincenzo sheet ("crosses the tax line, +$17.4M") told me what each move costs.
4. **Story texture.** "First win for the new coach", "The owner: I am proud of them. Tell them I said so.", the lottery reveal, news on Westbrook's Hall of Fame class.
5. **Speed.** Careers in 1-2 s, a week in 3 s, 80 games in 13 s. I never waited on the menus.

## What pushed me away (top 5, each marked dealbreaker / major / minor, with evidence)
1. **Jargon wall in the front office — major.** Re-sign page: "Bird rights", "Cap / Tax", "Their asks $31.3M", "rights let you pay $9.7M at most", "TO / PO", "Mid-level, one signing a season". ? icons exist, but I had to leave the flow to learn them. For a 2K player this is the point where I'd start guessing.
2. **A 40-point first game in the live game — major for retention.** Opener vs MIL, 132-92. The report said "luck" (-25.9) which is honest, but I had just spent 20 minutes setting things up and my first live game was a blowout I couldn't affect. Live-game tweaks (Switch, Q1 to Q2) never visibly helped in either career.
3. **Live games are very long in real time — major.** One quarter took about 3 minutes at 1x; a full game would be 12+ minutes. Skip ahead exists and is good, but a newcomer won't know which to pick, and the menu changes by quarter.
4. **Staff advice I couldn't read — minor to major.** "Sell to Philadelphia" meant trading my best player (87) for an 85 who is 30 on $183M x 3, with "forecast barely moves your chances". "Ride it out. Losing him hurts." The staff card tells me what, not enough about why.
5. **Owner goals and the staff's quiet moves — minor.** Welcome mail says "27 wins / under 21 is a failure", the goals mail says "26 / under 20" (it says opening night sets final numbers, but the first screen doesn't prepare you). In the fictional career the staff silently gave extra minutes to young players for the owner's goal "on a card you left" and it hurt, so I lost wins to a setting I never knowingly made.

## Against the genre (FM / BBGM / MyNBA / Hoop Land: where it wins and loses for me)
- **vs BBGM:** much more polished and phone-first, and it explains outcomes where BBGM only shows box scores. Loses on familiarity: I knew what to do in BBGM within a minute; here the cap sheet needed reading.
- **vs 2K MyNBA:** I actually watch the live game here and see why it went wrong, which 2K never tells me; but 2K lets me skip to what I want, and its menus use words I know.
- **vs FM (by reputation):** the "staff talking it over" and "while you were away" structure feels like FM's assistant manager. A newcomer will love that the staff handles things; the risk is I never learn what I'm even choosing.
- **vs Hoop Land:** not played; N/A.

## Would I pay and keep playing
- $7.99 phone unlock: yes. The first hour held me, and the season loop is easy.
- $14.99 on Steam: maybe. I'd want a better tutorial for cap and contract words first.
- Hours: I'd put in 15 to 25 hours, probably 2 to 3 seasons, then drift unless a story arc pulls me.
- Recommend to a friend: **7/10** for a basketball fan who likes explanations, 5/10 for a 2K friend who just wants to play.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Add a short, in-flow explanation of the cap and Bird rights on the Re-sign and Free agent screens (a one-line "what this means for you" under each number), plus a gentler first game: tell the newcomer before game 1 what the roster's weaknesses are (two F grades: three-point D and spacing) and what to try about them, so the first 40-point loss feels like a lesson, not bad luck.
