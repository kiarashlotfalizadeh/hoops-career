## What hooked me (top 5)
1. **Contract and pick fidelity.** Extensions, team options and protected picks with real-looking shapes (OKC's LAC/DEN/SAS 1sts).
2. **Honest game analysis.** Looks +26.4, shooting luck −17.4, free throws −8, and "Replayed without it: −0.4 pts ±0.6".
3. **The CBA bites.** Salary matching error text, the +$74M tax on a $14M signing, the tax line, Bird rights.
4. **Award rules and tributes.** The 65-game rule, retirement lines, franchise-record notices.
5. **Believable league texture.** Deadline recaps and trade grades, real-name FA signings and a plausible league environment.

## What pushed me away (top 5)
1. **Single-game scoring and one-sided outliers (major).** NYK 138, PHI 137 (275 points), a 64-48 half, and a 34-point home Game 7 blowout in a series that had been level.
2. **Flat free-agent pricing (major).** Almost every 77-78 player asks about $14M x 4. Diabaté's $75.9M is unrealistic.
3. **Real-data nitpicks (minor).**
   - Holmgren is listed PF.
   - Hartenstein is $23M against a real ~$28M.
   - "Murray" with no first name is ambiguous.
   - Team names like "GSW", "NOP" and "SAS" appear as the city.
   - The stray "Michael Porter Jr.." double period.
   - I can't verify Kawhi on TOR or LeBron on PHI.
4. **GM-only Coach of the Year (minor).** Odd for a GM-only role, though the line is cute.
5. **Confirm-trap UX (minor).** Release 2 and re-sign need two taps with no clear feedback. Live-game sheets ("Around the league") stay open and block the tab bar, and "Next" on the tutorial opens that sheet.

## Against the genre
- vs **FM**: the explanations (why you lost, what luck did) are at FM level. It has less squad management and no long-term club depth.
- vs **BBGM**: much more real-world texture and a better CBA. The sim is less transparent.
- vs **MyNBA 2K**: real data is as good, minus the licensed names. The story layer is better.
- vs **Hoop Land / Pro Basketball Manager**: modern UI and analytics, and the tax and rookie-scale details feel more current.

## Would I pay and keep playing
- $7.99 on phone: yes. $14.99 on Steam: probably yes if the sim variance is calmer and FA pricing gets a curve.
- Hours I'd put in: 40+, mostly on real-league careers.
- Recommend to a friend: 8/10.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Fix the sim extremes (275-point games, 34-point home Game 7s) and give free agency a real price curve, so the real-player trust the game builds in the first hour holds up over a season.
