# 02 — The Rebuilder
## Who I am and what I played
Chicago Stampede, 30th of 30 on the New League roster-rank screen. Real 2026 league, GM + Head Coach, job security on. Five full seasons (2026-27 to 2030-31), 410 regular-season games, three playoff runs, every offseason (lottery, draft, re-sign, free agency). Live games coached: one. I opened "Watch & coach" at Atlanta, watched about 30 seconds of Q1, then left. The game stayed resumable and I finished it as a quick sim. Phone viewport 390x844, 2D court. Sims were fast (a full regular season in 20-30 s, a deadline stretch in 10-20 s). About 6 hours of play.

## Play log
1. **Team pick, preseason.** The roster-rank line under each team ("Under each team: its roster's rank among the 30") made "pick the worst team" trivial. CHI was 30th. The owner opened with "win about 26 games, give 7,400 minutes to players 23 or under". Good hook: the owner's goal already read as a rebuild brief.
2. **Selling vets via the Trading block.** Shopping each player returns the best package from every team. Tre Jones went to Memphis for GG Jackson II (74, age 22) plus Hendricks. Jalen Smith went to Dallas for Max Christie (74, age 23). Norman Powell (33) went to Charlotte for two 2nds plus McNeeley. Collins went to LAL at the deadline for Vanderbilt plus two 2nds. Asking for a first for a 27-year-old 77 got "They want a lot more", and "What would make this work?" asked for a 2033 1st on top. That is honest, and it taught me what vets are worth: young players and 2nds, not firsts. The confirm sheet shows staff consequences: "Into the starting five: Smith. Minutes a game: Essengue 19 → 23".
3. **Owner adapts to the plan.** After the trades the owner's goal dropped from 26 to 17 wins and the youth-minutes target rose from 7,400 to 12,500. Preseason release prompts followed. Season 1 review: "A good season... Win 16 games — 21 wins (+25)". Tanking was rewarded, not punished. Job security never felt threatened. That was the most reassuring and the least tense part of the game.
4. **Year 1, 21-61.** I started 3-11 and was 11-40 at the deadline. The staff called "sell to LAL". The AI offers were lively (SAC offered Murray plus a 2028 1st projected #4-14 for Giddey). I declined because Giddey (24, 83/85) was the franchise guy. I finished 9-19 after the deadline, which cost me lottery position.
5. **Lottery, year 1.** 21-61 gave 14% odds. The reveal sequence is good ("Washington jumps from 7th to win #1", "You slip to No. 5"). I took Chuma Ibeh (76, POT 81-85), and he won Rookie of the Year. The draft's "Offers (27)" for the #5 pick (Mbeng, Johnson Jr., a #6 swap) felt real. Lottery luck went against me again in year 2: a projected #5 became #7.
6. **Year 1 re-sign shock.** Christie's ask went from $8.3M to $12.5M. Jackson II went from $2.41M to $14.8M after one season. The negotiation sheet (market value, interest level, slider, years, options) is superb. Signing needs a second tap ("signing can't be undone").
7. **Year 2, 28-54 with a good-looking roster.** Giddey 85, Buzelis 79, Wilson 79 and Essengue 78 sat at 13-26, 14th in the East. The Tactics screen explained it: A+ pick-and-roll D but D in rim protection after I had traded away every center. That is a real GM mistake, surfaced honestly. The Growth tab (rising and holding players, "staff priority", development coaching rank 8th) made development legible.
8. **Free agency lessons.** In the 2028 offseason I had $58.8M in space, then re-signed Buzelis ($26.1M for a 79/84) and Claxton, leaving $25M. Kyle Filipowski (C, 78/84) chose Brooklyn over my $18.2M offer against their $17.9M. The inbox explained why: "He rates BKN higher, for their market... Winning him would have taken about $18.3M". I had submitted at the ask and missed the "Lead with" button. By day 8 the market was bargains: Norman Powell (77) for $3.6M, Mo Bamba (74) for $5.8M a year.
9. **Year 3, 46-36 and Coach of the Year.** Summer development gave Essengue +5, Wilson +4 and Swain +3. At the Dec 11 stop I was 12-13, then went 29-20 by Jan 28 (18-8 in between). The owner's target had been recalibrated to 38 wins. 4th seed, lost 4-3 in round 1 to Charlotte after being down 2-1. Chicago got its first "Process gets past..." headlines the next year. That was the patience payoff I wanted.
10. **Year 4, 50-32, conference finals.** Mid-level Jarrett Allen (C, $13.6M, 22 rebounds in a playoff game) fixed the rim problem. The owner's season review: "Season grade +46... Two rounds further than I asked for". Other teams then offered me their head-coach jobs (Portland, Golden State). That is a great stakes beat.
11. **Re-sign crunch, 2030.** Wilson (85/88, age 23) asked $52.1M, Jackson II $30.1M, Swain $17.3M and Christie $14.2M. "Keep everyone" showed $308.0M and a luxury-tax bill of about $170M. I kept Wilson and Jackson II and let Swain and Christie go. That left payroll at $276.6M, $22.8M over the tax line. Salary-matching and AI offers made dumping Buzelis ($30.4M) impractical; every counter-offer sent back as much salary as I sent. I ate a $43.6M tax bill.
12. **Year 5, 44-38.** The owner grade was -2, almost entirely the tax goal (-92 on "Get under the luxury tax"). Giddey tore his meniscus and missed 33 games. I went 33-20 to 11-18 and finished 18th in the power rankings. Round 1 loss.

## What hooked me (top 5, each with evidence)
1. **A rebuild is a real, understood plan.** The owner re-set goals after my sell-off (26 → 17 wins, youth minutes 7,400 → 12,500), and the season review said "A good season" for 21-61.
2. **Lottery and draft drama.** "You slip to No. 5. Down 3 places from 2nd" and "Washington jumps from 7th to win #1" are painful and memorable.
3. **The Growth tab and summer development.** "Essengue +5, Wilson +4, Swain +3" plus a staff explanation of why. A 75/84 prospect became an 87 by year 5.
4. **The negotiation sheet and honest losses.** I lost Filipowski over a ~1% market-rating gap and the inbox told me exactly why and what I would have needed to pay.
5. **Cause-and-effect after the Allen signing.** The rim-protection weakness was named in Tactics (D in rim protection), I signed a center with the mid-level, and the team went from 28 wins to 50 inside two seasons.

## What pushed me away (top 5, each marked dealbreaker / major / minor, with evidence)
1. **Luxury-tax cliff after a successful rebuild (major).** Keeping Wilson, Jackson II and Giddey put me $22.8M over and cost $43.6M in tax. I could not dump Buzelis ($30.4M) because every offer sent back matching salary. A rebuild that wins should not end in a salary-dump trap with no cap-space buyers.
2. **Lottery balls burned me twice (minor).** I slipped from 2nd-best odds to #5 in year 1 and from 5th to #7 in year 2 (14% odds each time). Realistic, but it stung.
3. **Thin draft classes (minor).** The #20 and #24 picks were 65-ish OVR players who never played (Black 3 minutes a game). At #7 I hardly saw a center worth picking.
4. **Free-agency rules are opaque (major).** I did not realise "Lead with $X" was a separate button from "Submit offer", and "He weighs offers and decides on day 3" was only visible on the sheet. I lost my best target by $0.3M.
5. **The assistant-managed default meant I rarely coached (minor).** I never opened a game plan in five seasons ("No game plan yet" stayed on the Today screen) and the team still got to 50 wins.

## Against the genre (FM / BBGM / MyNBA / Hoop Land: where it wins and loses for me)
- **Vs Basketball GM:** The trading block, the explained AI offers and the owner mail beat BBGM's flat number-crunch. BBGM still has a deeper, more flexible draft board and easier mass-sim. This game's world feels more alive (news, retirements, coach-job offers).
- **Vs Football Manager:** The staff voices and "why" lines are FM-like, but FM gives you far more levers (training plans, scouting assignments). Here I could skip nearly everything and still win, which FM never allows.
- **Vs NBA 2K MyGM:** More honest numbers and a better cap model; far less spectacle. 2D court only in my test.
- **Vs Hoop Land / Draft Day Sports:** The negotiation sheet and re-sign market are better; rebuilds feel less scripted.

## Would I pay and keep playing
$7.99 phone unlock: yes, I would pay it for the franchise loop alone. $14.99 on Steam: probably, if the draft and FA depth grow. I put about 6 hours into five seasons and wanted a sixth. Recommend to a friend: 8/10.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Let me act on the luxury tax: show "who can absorb this contract" for a salary dump, or let the staff propose a tax-clearing deal. The owner grade (-92 on the tax goal) punished a success.
