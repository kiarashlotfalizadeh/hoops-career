# 04 — The Capologist and Trade Shark
## Who I am and what I played
Real league, Golden State Warlords (inherited $223.3M payroll, $22M over the tax line), **GM only**, 390x844 phone. Played 3 full seasons plus 3 full offseasons and entered a 4th preseason. Results: 2026-27 48-34, 5th seed, lost 4-2 in round 1. 2027-28 33-49, which gave the #2 pick. 2028-29 43-39, lost 4-2 in round 1. About 50 trades probed and 5 executed. 0 live games (GM only). Very long session; most of it was spent in Office/Trade/FA/Re-sign. I did not finish a 3-team trade (the screen opens, but I only inspected it). I could not open the draft-night "Offers (29)" overlay in the headless browser. A button click did nothing, so this is unverified, not a confirmed bug.

## Play log
- **Oct 2026, preseason.** The cap sheet and "How the cap works" are clear and correct: cap $165.0M, tax $201.0M, brackets of $6.1M at 1.5/1.75/2.5/3.25. My tax math matched ($48.2M on $22.3M over). The Trade screen shows a verdict meter, a "What would make this work?" button and a details panel with payroll after, tax saved and roster counts. I felt at home straight away.
- **Rules enforced on the first try.** Stepien ("can't be left without a first in back-to-back drafts"). "No team gives up more than 2 first-round picks in one deal." 125% + $0.3M matching, both ways. Roster 13-15. Recently signed players can't be traded until Dec 15. "Release Al Horford to make room" suggestions. Excellent.
- **Preseason cuts.** The staff told me to cut 2 and I signed Valančiūnas with the MLE. I cut 3 and paid $4.8M of dead money on two minimum deals. The rules text says "minimum deals for the coming season aren't guaranteed until opening night", yet Niang and Payton II ($2.4M) stayed as dead money. Terry ($1.36M) vanished with no dead money. Cost me roughly $17M of tax at 3.5x. See Bugs.
- **Dec 17 2026, first big fleece.** Butler (88, 37, $56.8M expiring, hurt) went to BOS. The Block screen offered P. George plus two 1sts. I asked for more and got **George (82) + Neemias Queta (82 C, 27yo)** for Butler + Toohey. The AI wrote "Butler (37) is worth more to a contender" and said "I will make the call to him myself. Deal." Even "Butler for George + 2 firsts" got a yes. Queta's trade card showed "$2.7M thru 30-31" but his deal steps to $12.5M next year. Still a huge win.
- **Feb 2027.** The offer queue contained lopsided inbound offers, e.g. ATL asked for Horford + Lendeborg + Bassey + 2 picks for Alexander-Walker. The "Why" panel said "a little short". The staff rated each offer "In your favor / A little short".
- **Offseason 1 (the Bird trick).** Paul George retired and his $54.1M salary came off the books. 27-28 cap space was $48.3M with no cap holds. Before re-signing anyone I took **Jrue Holiday (80, $37.2M) from POR for nothing**, into cap space. Then I re-signed Podziemski ($22.1M x5), Melton, Valančiūnas and Green ($2.54M, was $27.7M) **over the cap with Bird rights**. Payroll went from $48M of space to $205.8M. I broke a negotiation: a lowball got a counter at 90% of his ask, and a second lowball made him "firm" at +5% ($22.15M).
- **FA, waiting it out.** Day 1 to day 29 asks for vets fell: Powell 79 went from $9.7M to $4.3M, Smart $9.1M to $4.1M. The young 77-78s were gone by day 8. I signed Powell with the MLE at $4.3M.
- **Aging crash, summer 2027.** Curry 87→81 at 39, Queta 82→79 at 28, Powell -4, Holiday -7, Green -6. We started 20-33 and finished 33-49 with an 80-OVR roster. I sold Porziņģis (80, 32yo) to CHA for Allen + a 2029 1st at the deadline. Curry, Green, Horford and Payton II retired. Retirements cleared $60M+ of 28-29 money, so the 27-28 tax picture dissolved.
- **Summer 2028.** I landed the #2 pick and drafted Donovan Chapman (83, POT 96 at 19). He became 90 at 20 and 94 at 21. Cap space was $86M. **Jokić (95) hit the market** asking $64.9M x2. He "decides on day 3, bidding DEN", and the panel said "even a max offer would not top theirs". He chose DEN. The inbox explained why, in numbers ("rates DEN higher… same salary counts ~2% more").
- **Day 5 to 6, 2028.** The FA sheet shows the rival's best possible offer ("the most one could is about $26.4M") and when the ask falls. I waited, then signed **Davis (86) $29.8M, Jerome (84) $28.3M and Alexander-Walker (83) $26.3M, all 1-year**, with $84M of space that was mostly under the salary floor anyway. All three accepted. I then used the MLE ($5.4M) on Harris (79).
- **Summer 2029.** Re-signed Davis at $15.7M x1 and Harris. Opened FA with $82M. Signed Essengue (82/88, age 22, $39.9M x4) on day 1 and Tre Johnson (80/85) on day 5 at $23.1M x3, down from $27.9M x4. Finished with Gordon (78) and Bridges (76) on cheap 2-year deals. Payroll is $187.0M, under the cap, with Chapman at 94/99.

## What hooked me (top 5)
1. **The FA bidding explanations.** "Bidding: DEN. Even a max offer would not top theirs now", "No other team can pay his ask, the most one could is $26.4M", "From day 5 his price comes down, never under $27.8M". This is front-office play, not a slot machine.
2. **The Butler to George + Queta trade, with the AI reasoning visible.** A real fleece, and the game told me why ("worth more to a contender").
3. **Bird re-signs over the cap after cap-space moves.** A sequencing puzzle with a visible payoff ($48M of space became $205.8M payroll).
4. **The inbox explanations after losing a bid** (Filipowski: "CLE paid more, rates them higher for their record and market").
5. **Chapman.** 83 at #2, 94/99 two years later. Tanking once felt earned.

## What pushed me away (top 5)
1. **Major: some cap rules are friendlier than the CBA, and the game says so.** The taxpayer gets the full 8.5% MLE ($14.0M at $223M payroll; the real taxpayer MLE is about a third of that). Taxpayers also take back 125% + $0.3M in trades with no 100% limit and no aggregation limits. This makes a "taxpaying" team painless to run.
2. **Major: preseason cuts.** "Minimum deals aren't guaranteed until opening night" is false for $2.4M vets, which stayed as dead money. The staff's "cut 2" also became 3 once I signed one player, and I paid tax-multiplied dead money without a warning.
3. **Minor: the Trade screen shows only the current-year salary.** Queta shows "$2.7M thru 30-31" but steps to $12.5M. Raises are hidden in the one place a GM needs them.
4. **Major: aging is steep and uniform.** Curry -6 at 39, Queta -3 at 28, Moody -3 at 26. Buying 33+ players in FA is a trap every summer, and I walked into it twice. The game does flag it ("a year at that age moves a player -5 to +2").
5. **Minor: AI cap space vanishes by the deadline.** No team has cap space in-season, so dumps have no market after MEM absorbed Green. Sensible, but it makes the first dump the only dump.

## Exploits found
1. **Contender premium for old stars** (Butler for George + Queta; also "Jerome" fetches Towns, Reid + 1st, Sharpe etc.). The AI overpays vets that are about to expire. Cost-controlled young stars are cheap by comparison. Severe, repeatable each deadline.
2. **No cap holds + Bird after cap-space absorb.** Use cap space first (trade or FA), then re-sign your own players over the cap. Payroll goes from $48M of space to $205.8M. Rules text says "re-sign over the cap" but this ordering is the same exploit the real CBA closes with holds.
3. **Free good players from AI "negative value" contracts.** Holiday (80), George-like contracts. Salary-dump offers arrive unprompted (WAS offered Trae Young + a 2nd for nothing in 2029).
4. **Waiting out FA.** Vets lose ~50% of their ask by day 29, and the sheet tells you the rival ceiling so you can time a bid. 1-year deals avoid long commitments. Young players are gone by day 8 though, and Avdija vanished on day 6 even though his panel said rivals could only pay $14.8M against a $22.6M ask.
5. **Salary floor is free money.** The game says "You pay it anyway at season's end", so spending below the floor is free. I signed $84M of one-year deals with no luxury tax impact.
6. **Salary dumps are cheap.** Green ($27.7M expiring) plus a late 1st saved $48M of tax. The AI doesn't price tax for itself ("shedding saves them $0.1M of tax"), so a 1st is cheap.
7. **Retirements wipe contracts.** George's $54.1M thru 27-28 vanished. Aging stars on long deals are free insurance.

## Bugs (repro, no screenshot unless noted)
- **Podziemski re-sign total.** The confirm said "5 yrs $129.9M" at $22.15M, but the news said "5 years, $122.4M". Repro: Re-sign, Negotiate, lowball twice, accept his firm ask, then read the Jul 1 news.
- **"Mid-level: Used" shows while offers are pending.** Pending FA bids show the MLE as Used even though nothing was signed. It returns when the bids resolve.
- **Sub-minimum asks.** FAs asking $1.5-2.25M are labelled "Mid-level" and become unsignable once the MLE is used (Peavy $1.6M, Burton $2.25M), while $2.4M players show "Min deal". Players asking less than the minimum can't sign for the minimum.
- **Preseason release count.** The action said "Release 2", then "Release 3" after one signing. It cut Terry (not Niang) with no dead money. Dead money on non-guaranteed minimum deals contradicts the rules text.
- **Draft-night "Offers (29)" didn't open in the headless browser.** Could be my script; unverified.

## Against the genre (FM / BBGM / MyNBA / Hoop Land)
It beats BBGM on explanation: the AI tells you why it says no, and the cap rules are written in plain language. Beats NBA 2K MyGM on cap realism and FA bidding. It loses to a real CBA sim on cap holds, tax-line mechanics and taxpayer exceptions. It's closest to FM's contract talk (agent, "a little less may do") but with an auction. AI trade logic is better than BBGM's, because "no team gives up 3 firsts" and the Stepien rule are enforced.

## Would I pay and keep playing
$7.99 on phone: yes. $14.99 on Steam: yes if the CBA gaps close and a taxpayer MLE exists. I'd put 60-80 hours in, mostly offseasons. Recommend to a friend: 8/10.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Add real cap holds (or at least make the Bird re-sign period apply after FA cap-space usage) and shrink the taxpayer MLE. Right now the cleverest line in the game is also the one the CBA is built to stop.
