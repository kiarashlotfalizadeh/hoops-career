# 04 — The Capologist and Trade Shark
## Who I am and what I played
Golden State Warlords (real league, West), GM only (staff coaches). I picked them because they opened over the tax: $223.3M payroll, tax line $201.0M, a $48.2M bill, 17 players for 15 spots, and Curry (87, age 38, $62.6M) and Butler (88, age 37, $56.8M expiring) on the books. Three full seasons with all three offseasons: 43-39 (8th seed, swept 0-4 by OKC in round 1), 36-46 (lost the play-in), then 33-49 (12th). No live games coached, since this was a front-office run; the staff played every game. Phone viewport 390x844. Roughly 100 trade probes, 6 executed deals, 2 FA bidding fights, a 3-team builder session and about 15 re-sign and extension negotiations. Sim speed was fine: 2-8 s per month, one season in about 30 s.

## Play log
1. **Preseason, Oct 20 2026.** Cap sheet is clear: payroll bar with cap and tax ticks, over-the-cap $58.3M, tax $48.2M, a 17/15 roster flag, and a tappable "How the cap works" sheet. It states the real rules: 125% + $0.3M, brackets of $6.1M at 1.5/1.75/2.5/3.25x, repeater tax, Bird, Early Bird and the max formula. I never needed a wiki.
2. **First fleece attempt: Draymond ($27.7M expiring, 36) to ATL for nothing.** Refused with "Salary doesn't match: ATL takes in $27.7M against a $0.3M limit, the buffer alone with no salary sent." Honest and exact. "What would make this work?" then built a legal counter by adding Kispert and Finney-Smith coming back to me.
3. **Edge matching.** Draymond + Niang + Payton ($32.6M) for Beal ($25.8M) was blocked at $0.1M over: "takes in $32.6M against a $32.5M limit, 125% of $25.8M sent + $0.3M". Drop Payton ($30.1M) and LAC said "I will make the call to him myself. Deal." Executed. I dumped Draymond and Niang and took on Beal (74, thru 29-30), which I regretted later (see Bugs and Pushed me away).
4. **Star shopping.** Giannis (MIA, rebuilding, age 32, $58.5M): even Green + Porzingis + Podziemski + Moody + four firsts was "Not even close" ("I like a deal as much as anyone. Not this one."). Kawhi, Murray and Bane: all refused. The AI will not sell a "Franchise value" player for junk and picks, and "What would make this work?" added nothing. Stepien rule blocked a package of consecutive 1sts ("GSW can't be left without a first-round pick in back-to-back drafts (2027 and 2028)").
5. **Mid-tier buy that worked.** Moody + 2029 1st + 2031 1st for Nickeil Alexander-Walker (81, $14.4M thru 28-29, ATL rebuilding): the verdict ladder went "They want a lot more" (Moody alone), "Close: add a little more" (Moody + one 1st), "They'd take it" (two 1sts). I could price a first at about "half of what separates 76 from 81". Confirm sheet: "A first-round pick moves in this deal. Once it's made, it can't be undone." Fair, believable.
6. **FA, Oct 2026.** Valanciunas (76, $3.65M, 1 yr) used the whole mid-level. I tapped Make offer once at his ask and he signed. The cap impact line told me the truth: "+$11.7M tax" for a $3.6M signing, because the marginal rate is 3.25x.
7. **Owner goal flip (Oct 21).** The opening-night goal changed from "keep payroll under $223.9M" ("You inherited this payroll... won't count against you") to "Get under the luxury tax". After that trade I was $219M, $18M over, and no AI team could absorb salary (league-wide no cap space). I graded -100 on that goal at season's end; the overall season grade was +0 after 43 wins and a playoff berth.
8. **Deadline 2027.** 27-25. Staff offered a "Trade for Jaren Jackson Jr." call; AI offers to me were junk (UTA wanted Beal + Moody + Melton + 5 picks for Markkanen, 84). Their Markkanen counter needed Beal + Porzingis + Podziemski (80) + a 1st: no.
9. **Offseason 1: re-sign period.** Podziemski (80/84, age 23) asked $22.0M x5. I lowballed $15M: the ask rose to $22.1M, counter $21.45M ("less keen on your team now"). I lowballed again and it jumped to $23.65M "firm". Re-signs of Butler ($34.7M 1 yr), Melton, Valanciunas went fine. Extensions: Porzingis asked $17.2M (he earned $20.5M), Horford $2.7M. The game says the quiet part: "Open free agency? 3 players become free agents... You'd compete with cap space like everyone else." So no cap-hold exploit exists.
10. **FA bidding war (LaVine, 80, $14.5M).** The sheet showed "Bidding: MIN, TOR, +9", "decides on day 3", and "Leading takes $15.2M, more than the mid-level exception pays ($14.85M)". I offered the max $14.85M. Result: "Zach LaVine chose Minnesota over us. Their offer: 3 yrs at $14.7M. Our offer: 3 yrs at $14.8M." I outbid on money and still lost, with no stated reason. That is the one bidding message I distrust.
11. **Offseason dump window.** Beal had no takers all year ("No team has a package for Beal that works on value and salary right now"). In the first offseason MIL said it would take Beal + Miles Roberts (my #18 rookie, 73, POT 78-86) for nothing, saving $22.0M of tax. In-season the same deal became "They can't absorb that salary" and "GSW would drop to 12 players. The in-season minimum is 13." Dumps are only possible in July, and only by paying real assets.
12. **Season 2 (36-46) and 3 (33-49).** Aging cost me: Curry 87 to 83, Butler out, Davis/Randle declined as I probed them. I re-signed NAW at $30.4M x3 (he asked double his old salary), got Curry extended at $15.3M for 1 year (he earned $60.2M), and cleared the tax in season 3 ($214.2M, owner +23). Owner grades were -10, then +5. Job security stayed 48-50 ("can't fire you for 2 seasons").

## What hooked me (top 5)
1. **Every refusal told me why, with numbers.** "Salary doesn't match: LAC takes in $32.6M against a $32.5M limit, 125% of $25.8M sent + $0.3M" is exactly the edge-case answer I want. Also "GSW can't be left without a first-round pick in back-to-back drafts (2028 and 2029)" and "GSW would drop to 12 players. The in-season minimum is 13."
2. **The graded verdict ladder.** "They want a lot more" to "Close: add a little more" to "They'd take it", plus a one-line AI reason ("Moses Moody (24) is worth more to a rebuilder", "Picks count extra for them"), lets you binary-search a price like a real shark.
3. **Tax shown as a trade consequence.** "Saves $13.9M in 26-27 tax · $48.2M → $34.3M" on every trade, and "27-28 tax +$29.0M" on a $14.5M FA. The marginal tax math is the game for a capologist.
4. **FA bidding system.** Named rivals, decision on day 3, "What matters to him" (playing time, money, market, winning), and a hard cap on my bid by the MLE. It feels like a real market, not a button.
5. **Re-sign/FA structure.** The re-sign period before FA, no cap holds, "Over the cap is fine for your own players", and the plain warning "You'd compete with cap space like everyone else" make Bird rights meaningful and non-exploitable. Extensions priced below current salary (Curry $60.2M to $15.3M) reward planning.

## What pushed me away (top 5)
1. **(major) The league is cap-stuck, so contract dumps are almost impossible.** Every team I probed in 2026-27 failed with "takes in $X against a $0.3M limit" (BKN had $1.2M of room). The 125% rule alone means you can shed at most ~20% of salary per trade. A trade market where nobody can absorb salary, and AI payrolls sit near $190M-$232M, doesn't look like a real NBA July.
2. **(major) Owner goal "Get under the luxury tax" turned impossible after my first trade.** Preseason it was "payroll under $223.9M... inherited, won't count". After I trimmed $4M it flipped to "Get under the luxury tax" ($22.3M below) with no partner able to absorb, and it cost me -100 on that goal in season 1 and -72 in season 2 (weight 20%). The goal read as punishment for improving the roster.
3. **(major) FA bid lost with a higher bid and no reason.** LaVine: my $14.8M vs MIN $14.7M, he chose MIN. The sheet said MIN was "ahead today" at $15.2M but the final message just lists two offers where mine is higher. If non-money factors decided it, the message should say so.
4. **(minor) AI-built "What would make this work?" overasks.** For ATL it added a second 1st when Moody + one 1st was already "Close"; for WAS it said "I counted my picks twice". It isn't a fair-price finder; it is a maximum-ask finder. Sometimes it also silently does nothing when the deal is rule-blocked.
5. **(minor) AI trade offers to me are mostly bad.** UTA wanted "Horford + Lendeborg + 3 picks for Nurkic", "Beal + Moody + Melton + 5 picks for Markkanen". Sims stop for each, which is friction, and the "Your staff has you a little short on value" tag at least warns me.

## Against the genre (FM / BBGM / MyNBA / Hoop Land)
- **vs BBGM:** The CBA is simpler than real but much richer than BBGM's. BBGM's "what would make this deal work" is the same idea; here the reasons are in plain English and tied to the exact rule. BBGM's trade AI is fleeceable by roster-clog tricks; this one wasn't. BBGM lets cap-space teams absorb dumps constantly; this one doesn't, and that costs realism.
- **vs 2K MyNBA:** Much more honest. 2K trade AI accepts junk-for-star with enough picks; here Giannis never moved. The tax and matching explanations are far better than 2K's opaque "trade not allowed".
- **vs FM:** FM has contract clauses, agents and detailed transfer windows; this has a single day-3 FA decision and one agent-style slider. Not as deep, but the "How talks go" and "What matters to him" panels are the right shape.
- **vs Hoop Land / Draft Day:** Better trade UI than either; three-team trades with a "Make it work" builder is something none of them offer.
- **Where it loses:** no league-wide payroll or cap-space table, so I can't hunt for dump partners without probing 29 teams one by one, which is the first thing a capologist wants.

## Would I pay and keep playing
$7.99 phone unlock: yes, on the trade and cap screens alone. $14.99 on Steam: probably yes if a league payroll table and a trade-partner finder arrive; the front office is the strongest pillar for my type. Hours: 40-60 in a first month, then it depends on whether AI cap behavior changes. Recommend to a friend: 8/10 for a cap nerd, 6/10 for a casual coaching fan.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Give AI teams real cap management: some should carry actual cap space into July, and the trade screen (or a league tab) should show every team's payroll, tax status and cap space so the capologist can find a partner for a bad contract. Right now the best rules in the genre sit on top of a market where nobody can absorb a dump, so the clever tricks the rules invite have nowhere to go.
