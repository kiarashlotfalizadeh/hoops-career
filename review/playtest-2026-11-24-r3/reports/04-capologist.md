# 04 — The Capologist and Trade Shark
## Who I am and what I played
Real league, Golden State (West), GM only (staff coached), phone 390x844, headless. I took the highest-payroll team I could see ($223.3M, $48.2M tax bill, 17 on the roster) and played 2026-27 in full, 2027-28 in full, and 2028-29 to game 54 (deadline decision pending, 16-37). Results: 43-39 and a play-in win then a first-round loss to OKC (1-4); 38-44 and a play-in loss; 16-37. No live games coached, because GM only. Roughly 150 trade-screen probes, 3 completed trades, 2 offer acceptances, about 14 signings. Machine was shared and slow (scripts 1-2 minutes each), so I never got a full third season.

## Play log
1. **Preseason 2026-27, $223.3M.** The cap sheet showed: payroll, cap $165.0M, tax line $201.0M, $48.2M tax bill (over by $22.3M), roster 17/15, mid-level $14.0M, three years of committed money. Clear. The owner line was "keep payroll under $223.9M", which my inherited payroll already missed, and the game said so ("won't count against you").
2. **First dump.** Draymond (73, 36, $27.7M expiring, tagged Negative value) to Memphis (cap space $33.3M) for nothing: "Not even close". Green plus my 2033 1st: "They'd say yes", saving $48.2M of tax. Cost of dumping a bad expiring: one late first. I made it. The confirm sheet showed rotation impact and the pick I give up. Excellent.
3. **Salary matching at the edges.** Melton ($5.5M) for Carter ($7.6M): blocked with "GSW takes in $7.6M against a $7.1M limit, 125% of $5.5M sent + $0.3M." Adding Terry ($1.36M, $6.86M out) made it legal. This is the clearest rules explanation I have seen in a GM game.
4. **Fleece attempts, all refused with real reasons.** Butler + picks for Jokic: "Not even close". The AI said it rates what DEN would send at about 38 first-round picks like my best, and my whole pile at 2.5 ("each extra pick counts for less"). "Picks don't help them win now." Four players for Wembanyama: "Even I cannot make that one work... he counts 30% more to them." Three players for Markkanen got a real counter (I add a 1st). Stepien works: "can't be left without a first-round pick in back-to-back drafts."
5. **Three-team trade.** UI shows per-team boxes with a destination chip per asset, and each AI team's answer. "Make it work" produced a legal deal that asked for my best asset (Melton) plus a 1st for NAW + Carter. I did not take it. Mechanics fine, the counter was pricey.
6. **Deadline edge.** Moody + Lendeborg + 2029 1st for Bridges (80, $22.8M expiring) landed payroll at $200.9M, $0.1M under the $201.0M tax line. I did that deliberately. The owner review later paid it back: "Stay under the luxury tax: $200.9M, $0.1M under +15".
7. **AI offers to me.** About 8 over three seasons, always with a flavour line from the GM and a staff verdict ("A little short", "In your favor"). LAC gave Dunn (76) plus a 2nd for Santos (72): accepted, a free upgrade. CHI offered Powell (79, $22.6M option year) for pick #18 on draft night: accepted ("Trade grades: Golden State A, Chicago D" in the news feed).
8. **Re-sign period 2027.** Butler (37) asked $34.7M x1, down from $56.8M. Podziemski 80/87 asked $25.4M x5. Bridges: I lowballed (offered $15.0M against $18.25M) and his ask went UP to $18.55M and he called it "Disrespectful". The counter was $17.9M. I took it. Haggling saves ~3% at the cost of goodwill, so I would not haggle.
9. **Let-them-walk exploit failed, honestly.** Unsigned players "hit the market" and lose Bird rights. Melton (opted out) then showed as a regular MLE target with LAL leading the bidding. So you cannot re-sign over the cap after letting a player walk. The button text says so.
10. **Free agency 2027.** The best part of the game. Day 1 prices and a 3-day bidding window; "Bidding: LAL, DET, +13". The sheet says plainly when other teams can pay his ask and when the price drops ("From day 5 his price comes down, never under $14.9M. After day 20: $12.2M"). I waited on VanVleet (82), and he signed elsewhere. By day 27 the whole 76-78 tier was gone and only 67s and below remained at the minimum. I used the single MLE on Marcus Smart (78) at $5.0M, and the MLE was spent whole ("Used"). That is a trap worth a warning.
11. **Ageing hit the vets hard.** Curry 87 to 81 at 39, Butler 88 to 82, Westbrook 73 to 67, Horford 75 to 71. Curry's $55.7M plus $60.2M player option year is now toxic: Block says "No team has a package for Curry that works on value and salary."
12. **2028 shark combo.** Bridges (78, $18.8M to 2031) went to Sacramento for nothing (free, they said "Bridges is worth less to a rebuilder"). That opened $39.1M of space. I bid $34.3M, a hair above BKN's lead ($33.85M), for Anthony Davis (86, 34, 1 yr). "Anthony Davis chose us over BKN." Dump, clear space, win the star. That is the feel I wanted.
13. **Tail of the run.** Re-signed Butler (82) for $11.9M and Porzingis (80) for $14.8M, so old stars are very cheap. Added Kornet (76) for $3.2M and Mann (75) on the MLE. Still 16-37 at game 53 of 2028-29, 13th in the West.
14. **Owner goals did not fit my roster.** 2027-28 goals: "win 30 games" and "give 1,500 minutes to players 23 or under". I got +40 for 38 wins and -100 for 0 young minutes, final grade -9. 2028-29: "win 24" and "2,000 young minutes", after I signed AD and Butler. Brutal and contradictory with a veteran roster.

## What hooked me (top 5, each with evidence)
1. **The AI explains itself in numbers.** "38 firsts like your best one vs your side at 3.4, all your firsts in a pile = 2.5." I stopped trying to fleece and started reading.
2. **Cap rules shown inline.** "$7.6M against a $7.1M limit, 125% of $5.5M sent + $0.3M"; "Not enough cap space, $2.9M. Over the cap... up to $14.9M with your mid-level"; "Taking on $28.4M uses cap room they'd rather keep". No wiki.
3. **Free-agency pricing with a visible clock.** Decide day 3, price decays from day 5, floor stated, other teams' max shown. Winning AD with a dump-then-bid felt earned.
4. **The block and counters.** "What would make this work?" gives a concrete package; the block shows "Salary dump", "Fair offer", "Weak offer", and who values what.
5. **Trade confirm sheet.** Minutes shift, starting-five change, picks you give up, payroll after, in plain words.

## What pushed me away (top 5, each marked dealbreaker / major / minor, with evidence)
1. **Owner goals ignore the roster (major).** 2027-28: -100 on "1,500 minutes to under-24s" with Curry/Butler/Bridges. 2028-29 the ask went up to 2,000 after I added AD. Nothing in preseason says "your roster cannot do this."
2. **MLE trap and years trap (minor).** One MLE signing was burned on a $5.0M player (Smart), and minimum offers default to 3-4 years, so I got stuck with Hezonja, whose release cost $5.6M dead money. Warn before.
3. **AI cap management looks sloppy (minor to major).** About 17 of 30 AI teams sit over the tax in a fresh league; OKC carries about $251M. Believable for the real seed, but nobody ever dumps, and "Salary dump" offers to me show up in every season (Memphis kept sending Grant).
4. **Contract ageing is a cliff (minor).** Curry's untradeable $60.2M option year makes aged stars un-dumpable; combined with big rating drops, three seasons of vets went 43-39, 38-44, 16-37. Feels more punishing than the NBA.
5. **Slow big sims and blocking sheets (minor).** The "While you were away" and season-turn sheets blocked the next action when not dismissed; fine for a human, annoying for scripted play.

## Against the genre (FM / BBGM / MyNBA / Hoop Land: where it wins and loses for me)
- Versus **Basketball GM**: it wins on explaining AI valuations (BBGM just says no) and on the free-agency decision clock. It loses on trade volume: BBGM lets you chain endless small deals, here the 125% matching and the AI's price discipline make fleecing impossible, which is better but less fun for exploit hunters.
- Versus **NBA 2K MyGM**: far better reasons and rules text. Missing trade-exception mechanics and sign-and-trade.
- Versus **FM**: the Bird-rights/decision-day flow reads like FM contract talks, and the board flavour lines are good. I did not test the coaching side.

## Would I pay and keep playing
$7.99 phone unlock: yes, for the front office alone. $14.99 Steam: yes, if there were a desktop trade table and sorting. Hours: 25-40 for a cap nerd, more if the owner's goals fit the roster. Recommend to a friend, 8/10 for cap/trade fans, 5/10 for everyone else.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Make the owner's goals read the roster: don't hand a veteran-built playoff team "2,000 minutes for under-24s" with a -100 penalty. Or at least show a preseason line "your roster can't do this, here is the cost" and let me negotiate the goal.
