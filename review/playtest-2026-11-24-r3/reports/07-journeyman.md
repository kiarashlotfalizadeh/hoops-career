# 07 — The Career Journeyman
## Who I am and what I played
- Real league, GM + Head Coach, job security on, 390x844 phone viewport. I started with Chicago (East) and was told to win about 26 games.
- I played 10 full seasons (2026-27 to 2035-36). I stopped at the 2036-37 preseason. That is about 820 sim games, plus a full draft, re-sign and free-agency cycle each summer.
- I coached 0 live games. I watched only the 2D court in the sim stops, so I score Gameday N/A. Almost everything was Play-options sims, staff-call stops, trade offers, re-signs, the draft and the career layer.
- Jobs: Chicago for 5 seasons (38-44, 38-44, 51-31 with Coach of the Year, 36-46, 24-58 fired). Then Oklahoma City for 5 seasons (42-40, 45-37 with a conference finals run, 29-53, 26-56, 25-57). Career record 354-466.
- I ran `npm run season-test` once as supplementary evidence. I did not run star-check.
- Wall clock was about 3.5 hours. A season sims in about 5 minutes with my script handling the stops. Each stop is one click.

## Play log
1. **2026-27, opener.** The owner's goal sheet is clear and humane: "win about 26 games, give about 6,700 minutes to players 23 or under". The roster (Giddey 84, Powell 79, Smith 77) is better than a 26-win team. I went 38-44 and lost the play-in at Washington. Owner grade +43 because the bar was low. I liked that the bar was visible, but it felt soft.
2. **Offseason 1: the decline-then-re-sign trick.** Norman Powell (79 OVR, 23 ppg) had a $22.6M team option. The staff said "decline". Declining put him on the expiring list with an ask of **$10.7M x 2 yrs**. I re-signed him for less than half his option salary. It's a clean exploit. A hardcore capologist will love it, and a purist will think it's broken.
3. **Offseason 1: free agency.** The market has real bidding. Offers "decide on day 2", with rival teams named (HOU, MIN +15). Cap space is held for pending offers. I signed Fred VanVleet (82, $18.4M x 2) and bid the mid-level on Cardwell, and the day-2 reveal moment felt good. The cap/MLE UI text was clear.
4. **2027-28 and 2028-29.** The staff calls were lively: test-simmed counters, "Chicago is the biggest fraud in basketball" press lines, and trade offers with GM quotes. 38-44 again, then **51-31 and Coach of the Year in 2028-29** (lost in round 1). That was the high point of the Chicago run, and I felt the milestone moments ("Journeyman gets to 50", "reaches 200 career wins", "has beaten every team in the league").
5. **2029-30: 36-46, warm seat.** My roster aged (VanVleet 35, Powell 34) and I let it. The owner graded -60 and warned "two in a row and he fires you". That is a clear contract with the player.
6. **2030-31: deliberate tank.** I turned off the assistant and started the five worst players, with Fowler 86, Giddey 84, Essengue 83 and Wilson 80 on 0 minutes. The game stopped me with a blocking card, "5 regulars with no minutes — Give the staff's share / Keep them out", and an in-sheet warning on the rotation ("Fowler isn't getting any minutes"). I picked "Keep them out". The stars asked for trades ("He expects 32 minutes and plays 3.5"). Job security fell from 44 to 24 by December. I finished 24-58 and was **fired** with grade -100, exactly as warned.
7. **The job market (the best part of the game for me).** A "You're fired" moment, then "Find a new job". Four offers came in: TOR, OKC, POR and IND, each with the owner's reputation read ("Rated you 48, Eckert 49"). The Jobs tab shows why MEM passed on me ("Rated you 48, Forsythe 99"). The reputation card breaks down record, playoff teams and COY. I took OKC (the former dynasty) and got a "can't fire you for 2 seasons" clause. This is a real career layer, not a stub.
8. **After I left Chicago, the lottery gave them the #1 pick.** It was Dante Campbell, "a generational prospect" per the news. I think that is wonderful story luck. It also became the problem at point 13.
9. **OKC 2031-32 re-signs.** Fontaine (82/86, age 23) asked $29.8M. My Early Bird rights allowed $16.1M, so he left to free agency. Coleman (81/85) and Giannis (36, 79) also left. By 2032-33 the owner expected 30 wins and the roster had **12 signed, 13 needed**. The game forced me to sign a minimum player before the opener. Part of that is my autopilot, but the game never warned me that "Early Bird" meant "you will lose him".
10. **OKC 2032-33: 45-37, conference finals.** I won a first-round series 4-1 and a second-round sweep, with real signature wins ("13% underdog at San Antonio"). It felt earned.
11. **OKC 2033-36: three dead seasons (29-53, 26-56, 25-57).** The owner stayed patient ("3 seasons without the postseason is all he takes"). Grade -40 in 2035-36, one more and I'd be fired again. The 2036 #1 pick (Miles Webb, peak 90) was my consolation.
12. **League history check (year 5).** OKC won titles in 2026-27, 2027-28 and 2028-29 (SGA MVP and Finals MVP three years running, five MVPs in a row counting 2024-25 and 2025-26). That dynasty cooled naturally when SGA aged, and the league's champions then rotated: UTA, ATL, SAC, SAS, CHI, SAS, CHI. That is a healthy spread.
13. **League history check (years 8-10): the world drifts at the top.** Two draftees, Sam Dunn (SAS, class of 2031, 99 OVR at 23) and Dante Campbell (CHI, 99 at 22), produced **Campbell: 44.8 ppg, 2,911 points in 65 games, 48.7 ppg in the Finals**, and **Dunn 39.1 ppg**. Chicago went 67-15 at 127.3 ppg, SAS went 73-9 and 72-10 at +16.7 net rating, and MIN went 10-72. The league average stayed flat the whole time. The extremes are the problem (details below).

## What hooked me (top 5, each with evidence)
1. **The career layer.** Being fired at 24-58 with a "-100" grade, four offers, a reputation of 48 ("17th of 30 head coaches") and a visible reason why Memphis passed ("Forsythe 99") made me want to see the next job. It is exactly what I came for.
2. **The honest owner contract.** Goals appear on opening night, and the warning "two in a row and he fires you" was kept to the letter. The game never rubber-banded my job security.
3. **A living league that remembers.** LeBron retired after 24 seasons with 44,861 points. Curry retired after 20 seasons (31,334 points), Durant, Butler and Hield said goodbye in order. The Hall of Fame has 17 inductees by 2036. Records appear ("Adebayo 83 points", "CHA 33 threes"). I read these pages for fun.
4. **Free agency has stakes.** Day-2 decisions with named rivals, held cap space and Bird rights text made each summer a small event (VanVleet signing, the Powell re-sign).
5. **Moments that name me.** "Journeyman has beaten every team in the league", "Journeyman reaches 200 career wins", the Coach of the Month and COY honours, and signature wins listed with the underdog %, are the kind of FM-style career trivia I treasure.

## What pushed me away (top 5, each marked dealbreaker / major / minor)
1. **MAJOR: top-end inflation by year 8.** Campbell 44.8 ppg and 2,911 points, Dunn 39.1, Finals MVP at 48.7 ppg, and 99/99/98/97 OVRs in 2036 (versus a 97/95/95 top end in 2028). SAS went 73-9 and then 72-10 (+16.7 net). MIN went 10-72. The league mean never drifted, but the tails grew. A year-20 save would have 45-ppg scorers normally. That is the one thing that would stop me playing at year 10.
2. **MAJOR: the "generational prospect" generator.** The news announced "a generational prospect" for the 2029 class (Ty Crawford, 7'3") and again for 2031 (Dante Campbell, 6'7", "the kind of prospect who comes along once in a decade"), two years apart. Every other class also had a 90+ star (Dunn, Crawford, Fletcher, Boozer, Peterson). "Once in a decade" is not once in a decade.
3. **MAJOR: Early Bird cap silently loses young stars.** Fontaine (82/86, 23) asked $29.8M. My Early Bird allowed $16.1M, so "No offer can re-sign him". It is correct CBA, but the Re-sign page's headline "Bird rights: keep them" lures you into thinking everyone is safe. There is no warning before you let a 23-year-old 82 walk.
4. **MINOR/MAJOR: option-decline discount.** Declining Powell's $22.6M option and re-signing at $10.7M is a cheap loophole, and the AI has no equivalent.
5. **MINOR: sheet blocking after sims.** After a sim, a result sheet (a "dg-line" diagnostic or "While you were away") sits over the Continue bar. Tapping Play options fails until I press Escape. A real player would tap the backdrop, but it cost me several script failures.

## Bugs (repro steps, screenshot path)
- **Post-sim sheet intercepts the Play options button.** Sim to the deadline (Feb 6, 2027), then tap the Continue bar arrow. The "Test-simmed Stay home in place of Balanced..." dg-line sheet overlaps. Escape clears it. Screenshots: players/07-journeyman/s5.png and s20.png.
- **Re-sign "Negotiate" with no Bird rights has no offer button.** (Fontaine, 2031.) The sheet says "No offer can re-sign him", which is correct but a dead end. Screenshot: s24.png.
- **Preseason Start is blocked by queued cards in an order that isn't obvious.** "Release 2 players", "Keep them out" and "Sign 1 player" each need a two-tap Confirm before Start works. It works, but it reads as a hung button the first time.
- No page errors were collected across the whole run (`page.errors` was empty every time).

## Against the genre (FM / BBGM / MyNBA / Hoop Land: where it wins and loses for me)
- **Beats BBGM on the career spine.** BBGM has no job market, no reputation, no "agent brings you offers". This game's Jobs/Reputation/Openings screens are Football Manager-level. It also has plausible narrative text where BBGM has numbers only.
- **Beats BBGM on contract texture.** Early Bird, Bird rights, mid-level, bidding and day-2 reveals make summers matter more than BBGM's single-click re-signs.
- **Loses to BBGM on long-run balance.** BBGM's 100-year sims are famously flat. Here, year 8-10 already has 44-ppg seasons and 99s.
- **Loses to FM on scouting and tactics feedback.** My tactics questions mostly returned "No clear edge". I never felt a tactical lever that moved a season.
- **Versus MyNBA:** the history pages (HoF, records, coaches table with 30 coaches) are far better than anything 2K ships.

## Would I pay and keep playing
- **$7.99 phone unlock:** yes. The 10-year loop is cheap to run, and the career layer is a genuine reason to open the app daily.
- **$14.99 on Steam:** only if the star inflation is fixed and live coaching is as good as the career layer. I'd wait for a patch.
- **Hours:** about 30-40 on phone before I'd feel the world warping. I would play past year 10 only after the top-end fix.
- **Recommend to a friend:** 7/10.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Cap the talent tail. Keep generational draftees truly rare (one per 8-10 years, not five in a decade), and bring their ceiling to about 95. Their 39-48 ppg lines and 72-10 team seasons are what made year 10 feel like a different game than year 1. The league mean never moved, so a tail cap is the whole fix.
