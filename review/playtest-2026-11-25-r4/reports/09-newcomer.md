# 09 — The Crossover Newcomer (19, 2K MyNBA player, one BBGM tab)

## Who I am and what I played
- Career 1: Washington Mages (real rosters, default pick), GM + Head Coach, Teaching ON, all default toggles. One full season (2026-27: 30-52, 13th East, missed the play-in), then the lottery (pick 9), draft, re-signing, free agency, and the start of 2027-28.
- Career 2: fictional league, LA Cutters (West), GM + Head Coach, defaults. 21 games played (10-10, 7th West, Dec 2).
- Live games coached: 1 (the opener, to overtime, a loss). 390x844 phone, headless. Roughly 3 hours of wall time, about 25 in-game "sittings".
- Caveat: the live court showed 3D in the first screenshots, which looked far better than I expected from a PWA. I switched to 2D to keep things fast.

## Play log
1. **/start (min 0).** Hoops Career title card, one big button, "New league". Three steps, each a single screen. I understood everything. Real vs Fictional, then team cards with roster rank ("21st of 30"), then role, Injuries, Job security and Teaching toggles. Teaching was ON by default. Easiest setup I've seen in a management game.
2. **Team card oddity.** Washington's step-2 card says "Owner: 27 wins · rebuild · frugal". Step 3 says "27 wins · rebuild · win-now". Those contradict. I paused and wondered which was true. The owner message later reads like a rebuild, so "win-now" looks like the bug.
3. **Today screen (min 2).** The owner's three goals sit in the hero card: win 27, give 10,000 minutes to players 23 and under, stay under the tax. Hooked, because I knew what "good" meant on day one. "10,000 minutes to players 23 or under" took me two reads. Nobody in 2K asks that.
4. **The guide checklist (min 3-12).** "Your first games, 0 of 4": rotation, playbook, plan for the opener, coach a game live. Each step opened the right page with a banner and a one-tap "Keep these five / Keep this playbook / No plan this time". This is the best onboarding I've seen in a sim. I finished 3 of 4 in about 4 minutes without understanding most of it. "Drop coverage", "4-Out, 1-In", "Post Hub" and the default tactics grades (F in Three-point D, F in Spacing) went right past me. I didn't feel stupid, because every step had a "keep the staff's" exit.
5. **Plan for your opener.** The three "Counters" all said "Staff still looking…" with an Add button, so I had nothing to add and bailed with "No plan this time". The "Who guards whom" list is great (it shows "Ball handling 85 vs perimeter defense 69" in red or green), but I only half understood it.
6. **Live opener vs MIL (min 15-40).** Big win for hook. Starting lineups, a "the game is a broadcast: it plays sideways" prompt, a real-looking 3D broadcast court, "To win MIL 44% / WAS 56%" bar, a staff coach card, and 4-step coachmarks (speed, court, timeouts, "Players has subs, Now has staff calls"). The end-of-quarter break is the best part: shot chart, FG/3P/rebounds/turnovers bars, and "What's deciding it: MIL's shots fell above their looks... luck, with nothing to change". Down 24-33, I tapped Tactics and found "What MIL is running: Spot-up, 18 points on 8 trips, Hurting you", with buttons right there (Help defense: Stay home / Balanced / Pack paint). I tapped Stay home. The "Your moves" log then showed "WAS 112-103 since". That is BBGM clarity plus 2K feel. I felt clever.
7. **Late game.** Tied 136-136 at the end of regulation, with a "Last possession, your ball" screen and "Call the play" options (Iso: Young / Chicago: Young / Post split: Davis). I picked Chicago. Overtime came. The staff suggested a 2-3 zone, I accepted, and the game later told me "Milwaukee's zone clogged driving lanes... WAS 5-11 since". I lost 141-147ish. That was a story, not a button press. I was invested.
8. **Scoring sanity.** Final line 136-136 after 48 minutes, 57% FG vs 53%, 13-34 and 16-41 from three. Other scores that day: 130-107, 132-102. Real NBA is about 115. A 2K kid won't care, but my BBGM-trained eye does: it reads like an arcade game.
9. **First week (Oct 22-28).** "While you were away" card: record, rank move, results, one story, mail. Quick to read. 2-3 after week 1. I hit "Sim 1 month" and went 1-6 in the stretch (3-9). Trae Young got hurt. The staff note explained "Outscored by 4.0 a game. Some of it is shots that didn't fall. That's luck, not the plan." The mail showed "The owner: six straight losses". A newcomer might be annoyed, but the game says why. First near-quit: **sim 34 games to Jan 23 and I was 16-30.** I had done nothing in those months. Nothing asked for me, so I played the sim, not the game. That is where a 2K player would drift to YouTube.
10. **The trade call (Nov 12).** "SAS want George for Reed Jr. + 1 pick." "Why" said "Staff: in your favor. Your staff has you ahead on value." That told me nothing: ahead how? Accept needs a second "Confirm" tap (I only tapped once, so the offer lapsed). Fine and safe, but I learned nothing about George's worth.
11. **Making my own trade.** Office -> Trade is great: team cards, tags like "All-Star value / Starter value / Negative value", contract lengths, picks with projections. I asked for ATL's J. Johnson (88). The game said Not even close and "Salary doesn't match: WAS takes in $30.0M against a $0.3M limit". "What would make this work?" built a counter: Dybantsa + Sarr + three firsts + a 2029 first + $7M cash. Honest, but I'd never have guessed the salary rule without the red text. The game taught me the CBA by saying no.
12. **Deadline "sell" call.** The staff suggested sending Davis (87) to Boston for Neemias Queta (82) + Paul George (82), "no clear edge from the deal", on a 17-35 team. I said stand pat. Sell advice that returns two older, worse players made me trust the staff less.
13. **Season end.** 30-52, missed the play-in, but the owner goal (win about 26) was met. "Your season is over" was handled gracefully. Sim to end of playoffs went through to the **lottery**: "Reveal the order", lottery balls drawn from 14th up, and "You slip to No. 9. Down 1 place from 8th: the balls didn't bounce your way." SAS jumped from 10th to #1 (3.0% odds). This was a highlight. I screamed at my phone.
14. **Draft (pick 9).** On the clock card, prospects with OVR, POT ranges and "staff read", a "Make the pick?" confirm sheet, auto-draft the rest. I took Chance Thompson (73, POT 84-86). That was fun. I couldn't judge "Combo Guard" or "Fair · ranked 9th" though. I had no idea what a scouted rank meant.
15. **Re-signing + free agency.** I exercised rookie options, negotiated Coulibaly (77/82) at his ask, and tapped through "Confirm: sign him · signing can't be undone". Free agency: sorted by OVR, "Chaney Johnson 78/82 at $9.1M", a bid with "Bidding: LAL, DEN, +23... decides day 3" and "Lead with $9.65M". I bid, advanced a week and got "Chaney Johnson chose us over LAL, DEN, ORL and CLE and 18 more". This is the best moment in the whole first-career loop. It feels like winning.
16. **Summer development.** Preseason 2027-28: "Dybantsa +4" (85/92 at 21), Davis down to 84 at 34, Ament up 5 to 80 for another team. You see your kid grow. That is the hook for rebuilders and 2K "draft class" players.
17. **Fictional league (21 games).** Same flow, same guide, 4 sims of a week each. LA Cutters, generated names (Jamal Curry 86, Yuki Tominaga 83...). 10-10 after 21. Leaders: Darius Gray 35.2 ppg, top five above 31. The stories are the same quality ("LA Cutters edges LA Lagoons 123-121 at the finish"). But I had no emotional attachment to any name except my own team.

## What hooked me (top 5)
1. **Free agency bid that I won.** "Chaney Johnson chose us over LAL, DEN, ORL and CLE and 18 more: 4 yrs at $9.7M" — a visible market with rivals and a decision day.
2. **The lottery reveal.** "You slip to No. 9... San Antonio jump from 10th to win the No. 1 pick" with odds listed per team.
3. **Quarter-break "What's deciding it".** "That part is luck, with nothing to change" is the honesty I'd never get from 2K, and the "Hurting you" tab let me act on it in one tap.
4. **The first-games checklist** "Your first games · 1 of 4 done" with "Keep the staff's" on every step.
5. **Owner letters and the story layer.** "Six straight losses... Washington is grumbling about this team" and the moments card ("Johnson wins it late for Washington") gave a 16-30 season a shape.

## What pushed me away (top 5)
1. **Long quiet sims (major).** "Sim to trade deadline" went from 3-9 to 16-30 across 34 games with 0 decisions. A newcomer who doesn't know what to tweak will just sim, lose, and quit. The game only stopped for injuries, offers and the deadline.
2. **Jargon on defaults (major).** "Drop coverage", "4-Out, 1-In", "Post Hub", "Bird rights", "mid-level", "over the tax line", "No Bird rights: up to $9.7M". Hover "?" exists, and the glossary is good, but the default screens throw 20 terms at once. F grades in Three-point D and Spacing on the team I'm told to "keep" is scary.
3. **Scoring looks arcade (major for BBGM fans, minor for 2K fans).** 136-136, 57% FG, 16-41 threes, 35 ppg leaders. I'd tell a purist friend this isn't an honest sim.
4. **"Why" on staff calls and trades is thin (minor).** "Your staff has you ahead on value" doesn't say how. The deadline "sell" call returned two older 82s for an 87.
5. **Fictional league has no hook beyond my team (minor/major for 2K fans).** "Jamal Curry 86" means nothing to me. No Wemby, no Luka, no real-league stories ("Dončić wins his first MVP" was the best news line in career 1).

## Against the genre
- **vs BBGM:** much clearer teaching, much better presentation, and the "what they're running / hurting you" tab is something BBGM never had. BBGM is faster to sim and its scoring is more believable.
- **vs NBA 2K MyNBA:** this wins on explanations, owner stakes and the free-agent bidding model. It loses on flash: no real-player faces (my team had generic ones), no dunk packages. The 3D court was the only moment that felt like 2K.
- **vs FM:** I can't judge depth. The structure (staff calls, ownership letters, scouting reads) feels FM-like and approachable.
- **vs Hoop Land / Draft Day:** the tone is more modern, more readable on a phone.

## Would I pay and keep playing
- $7.99 phone unlock: yes, if the free part lasts through one season. The lottery and free-agency loops made me want to see year two.
- $14.99 on Steam: maybe. I'd want real names and faster live games.
- Hours: 15-25 hours before I drift, mostly in rebuild-and-draft seasons.
- Recommend to a friend: 7/10 for a 2K player who likes MyNBA, 6/10 for a casual.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Cut the quiet stretches. Make long sims stop at a "this month's one thing" card (for example: "You lost 6 straight; try X", with a one-tap suggestion), and give default tactics a plain-English line ("We run 4 shooters and a big; our weak spot is three-point defense"). That would close the gap between "I kept the staff's" and "I'm playing the game".
