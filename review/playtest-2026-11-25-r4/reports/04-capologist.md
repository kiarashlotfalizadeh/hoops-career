# 04 — The Capologist and Trade Shark
## Who I am and what I played
Golden State (real league, West), GM only, so the staff coached. I played 3 regular seasons and two full offseasons: 53-29 and a West semifinal loss in 7 (2026-27), 44-38 and a play-in loss (27-28), 36-46 (28-29, played to the end of the regular season; I did not play out the third offseason). No live games, because my game is the front office. Phone, 390x844, headless. About 90 scripted sittings, which was mostly the trade and free-agent screens.

## Play log
1. **Oct 2026, day 1.** I took the roster at $223.3M against a $201.0M tax line, with 17 players for 15 spots. The owner's note said the inherited payroll "won't count against you". The cap sheet opens in one tap and has a "How the cap works" page. It covers the mid-level, Bird/Early Bird percentages, the max by years of service, tax brackets (1.5x, 1.75x, 2.5x, 3.25x), the repeater rule, 125% + $0.3M matching, releases and dead money. I didn't need a wiki. Felt clever.
2. **Fleecing attempts, all refused with numbers.** Green plus 7 firsts for Jalen Johnson: refused. The AI answered "ATL rate what they'd send at about 10 first-round picks like your best one, and your side at 4. All of your firsts in one pile come to 3.8 for them (each extra pick counts for less)." The Stepien rule also fired ("can't be left without a first-round pick in back-to-back drafts"). Four firsts plus Podziemski for Johnson: "Not even close". Moody plus 2 firsts for Avdija: refused. Butler for Giannis: "I do not get out of bed for that." Junk for Kawhi: refused. I could not buy a star with junk and picks. That is the best thing in the game for a shark.
3. **Dumping Green ($27.7M expiring, 36yo).** Memphis (cap space) wanted a first for him. Two seconds weren't enough. I gave the 2033 1st. Payroll went from $223.3M to $195.6M, and the tax went from $48.2M to $0. The confirm sheet showed the staff's rotation read, who moves into the starting five, and "you give up your 2033 first". At the end of the year I received a $17.2M share of the league tax pot.
4. **Extensions as the cheapest value in the game.** Butler (88 OVR, $56.8M expiring) asked $28.45M for 1 year, and I signed it. Podziemski cost $18.1M x5 and Williams $5.9M x4. Haggling at 88-92% of the ask gets a counter about 6% under the ask for free. A second lowball in a row gets you shut out: "Talks are off... until Oct 27: two lowballs were too many" (Valanciunas). The ask also rose and his interest dropped. I felt punished, fairly.
5. **Edge-of-125% matching.** Horford ($6.8M) for Wiggins ($9.2M) was blocked with "GSW takes in $9.2M against a $8.8M limit, 125% of $6.8M sent + $0.3M." A just-inside version passed the rules check. Every cap refusal states the exact limit, and the screen never leaves you guessing.
6. **"What would make this work?" is a deal solver, and it is greedy.** Asking for Pippen Jr. made it add a 2031 1st. Asking to dump a $6.4M player made it ask for Podziemski (81 OVR, 24yo, my best asset) in the 3-team screen. For Queta (82 OVR, $2.7M) it asked for Curry plus three firsts plus $7.5M cash. It also overwrites your deal when you tap it. Nobody is hurt, but it reads as absurd.
7. **First-week offers.** UTA wanted Horford, Lendeborg and a 1st for Nurkic. LAC wanted Porzingis plus Moody for Ingram. Most AI offers ask me to overpay. In the one offer I got (PHI: Melton for Simons + 2nd), my staff called it "even on value". A 1-month sim ran through the LAC offer (stood until Nov 27) before I could answer it.
8. **Season 1 on the floor.** 0-4 start with Butler (ACL) and Porzingis out, 11-7 by Nov 27, 36-19 at the deadline, 53-29 and 5th seed. The deadline staff card suggested trading Porzingis, Moody, Williams and a 1st for Kyrie Irving while saying "The deal could hurt." I stood pat. Lost the semifinals 3-4 (game 6, 147-119 loss).
9. **Free agency 1 (Day 1 of 30).** My first mid-level bid on Mathurin lost by $0.1M: CLE offered $14.9M and my cap was $14.85M. The inbox told me exactly that and said winning him would have taken $15.2M. The mid-level came back after the loss, and I got Nurkic (80) with it. Players "decide on day 2-4" and weigh playing time, money, market and winning, and the sheet tells you which ones matter. "Lead with $12.35M" buttons give the minimum winning bid. This is the best FA system in the genre.
10. **Offseason 2: cap-space round trip.** I dumped Nurkic ($13.0M, 78 OVR, 33yo) on the Cutters ($66M space) for a 2nd. Pressing "Propose" on a refusal gave me three versions: take Dean Wade's contract, add $2.2M cash, or add a 2nd. That pushed my cap space to $32.4M, and I signed Anthony Davis (85 OVR, 34yo, $29.0M x1). The cap space was "held" for the pending bid. At the 28-29 deadline I flipped Davis to Detroit for Collins, Huerter, a 2031 1st and two 2nds. That is a cheap free-agent-to-trade arbitrage that works every summer.
11. **Pick swaps.** Swap rights are priced in plain probabilities: "That's about 5% likely today. It's worth about 4% of your pick to them." I never executed one (the AI wanted a real asset for Beringer), but the transparency is unmatched.
12. **Three-team trades.** The UI is clear (per-asset destination chips, a verdict per team, "Blocked by the rules" with a reason like "SAC would have 22 players. The most is 21"). I never landed one: "Nothing you have gets both teams to yes." Wolf (78 OVR, $3.1M) cost Santos plus a 1st plus $1.7M cash in a plain two-team deal, which I did.
13. **Season 3 and the owner.** 36-46 at 40yo Curry (83 to 79 OVR). The owner target dropped from 37 to 38 to 32 wins as my record fell, so the "wins" ask tracks the roster and not my record. Butler retired after 2027-28, between seasons, and I was not warned in the re-sign room ("No expiring contracts this summer").

## What hooked me (top 5)
1. **The AI will not be fleeced, and it says why.** "All of your firsts in one pile come to 3.8", "worth about 4% of your pick to them", "Picks count extra for them". Every refusal had a number and a reason I could act on.
2. **The cap explainer and the rule messages.** "125% of $6.8M sent + $0.3M" and "its $33.3M of cap space + $0.3M" were exactly the limits I was testing. Trade confirm sheets also show tax saved/added ("$48.2M -> $37.3M") and the staff's rotation read.
3. **FA bidding with day-2 decisions.** Rivals named, "What matters to him" shown, held cap space, minimum-winning-bid buttons, prices that fall after day 5 ("never under $10.8M. After day 20: $8.7M"). I lost Mathurin by $0.1M and the game told me so.
4. **Extensions and Bird rights have real texture.** Butler's 1-year $28.45M, the 6% haggle counter, the "two lowballs" shutout, "A rotation player walks when free agency opens, unless you re-sign him first."
5. **Refusal alternatives.** "Propose" on a rejected dump returned three accepted versions (Wade's contract / $2.2M cash / a 2nd). I used it.

## What pushed me away (top 5)
1. **(Major) The make-it-work solver asks for your best player.** To dump a $6.4M role player it asked for Podziemski; for Queta it asked for Curry plus three firsts plus $7.5M cash; and tapping it silently rewrites your deal. A shark stops trusting a button like that. Evidence: /tmp-run screenshots 55-3team-b.png and the Queta ask in my log.
2. **(Major) AI payroll discipline is weak.** In 2026-27, 13 of 30 teams were over a $201.0M tax line (OKC $246.6M in the 27-28 summer, $246.9M in 28-29 while 65-17; PHI $228.9M, TOR $232.1M). Teams that use their space do spend it within days (CHI went from $35.5M of space to $0.3M over in the summer), which is believable. But a 14-team tax club means the tax doesn't bite. A shark can't pin "bad cap management" on anyone.
3. **(Minor/Major) AI-initiated offers are mostly asks, not gifts.** UTA, LAC, PHI, ATL, SAS, OKC, IND all wanted a good player for equal or worse value. A 1-month sim skipped past one (LAC) before it showed up on Today ("1 trade offer" only in the recap). Offers are the only way the AI exploits me, and they never made me think.
4. **(Minor) No cap-hold exploit and no tax pain.** Re-sign closes when FA opens, so the Bird-rights ordering trick is blocked, which is fine. But because I could dump contracts for a 2nd/cash and run a $30M-under-tax roster, the tax mattered only for the owner-goal weight (15%). I never faced a real tax bill.
5. **(Minor) Small honesty/UX nicks.** "They want a lot more" over "Close, but it is not even." ATL's offer card hid that Sharpe was "Out 61 games" (I saw it only in the roster list). The release explainer says camp deals "cost nothing" while Niang's min deal cost $2.4M dead money. A salary floor line appeared ("$0.9M under the salary floor") that the cap explainer never mentions. Owner wins goal fell to 32 after a 44-win year.

## Against the genre (FM / BBGM / MyNBA / Hoop Land)
- **Versus BBGM:** BBGM's trade AI is a fixed value formula you can feel your way around; here the AI explains itself ("worth about 4% of your pick") and the pick-pile cap stops the classic dump-ten-picks-for-a-star trick. It wins. BBGM's player progression and league weirdness (AI managing the cap badly) is a smaller gap.
- **Versus MyNBA/2K:** 2K's trade AI is a joke by comparison. This one wins easily; 2K has better FA stars (this class topped at 85 and few stars ever reach FA).
- **Versus FM:** FM's contract haggling (agent patience, clauses, "slightly under market") is the closest cousin, and the lowball lockout is a nice nod. FM wins on contract clause depth (options, bonuses); this wins on how clearly it explains the rule that blocked you.
- **Versus Hoop Land / Draft Day:** Better UI than either, thinner league simulation of AI GM behaviour.

## Would I pay and keep playing
$7.99 on the phone: yes, immediately, on the strength of the transaction layer. $14.99 on Steam: yes if the AI-GM layer deepens; as is I would call it a strong phone game and an average PC one. Hours: about 30 to 40, two to three more seasons of the same loop (extend, dump, bid, flip) before I'd want a star-FA or contract-fight twist. Recommend to a friend: 8/10 for cap nerds, 6/10 for general fans.

## The one change that would most raise my score
Make the AI teams feel like GMs: let them pay a real tax and get pinned by it (so the taxpayer club shrinks, AI dumps appear on the block with honest "salary dump" asks I can exploit or refuse), and have the make-it-work solver stop reaching for your best player. That turns the already-honest trade screen into a cat-and-mouse game instead of a locked door.
